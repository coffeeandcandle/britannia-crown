Homework is a source of tension in many families. Schools set it differently, and what is right depends on age and need.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **There is no national rule**, so check your school's policy.
- At primary, homework is usually **short and light**, often reading and practice.
- If it is causing distress, **talk to the teacher**.

</Callout>

## Primary school

Primary homework usually includes regular reading, times tables or spellings, and sometimes short tasks. It tends to be a few short activities a week, rather than long sessions.

## Secondary school

Homework increases with age, and by the GCSE years can be substantial, including revision. Schools often set a timetable. Ask the school about expectations.

## What helps

- A regular time and quiet place.
- Starting with the hardest task if energy is high.
- Short breaks.
- Parents available to help, without taking over.

## When it is too much

Signs include tears, late nights and regular conflict. Speak to the teacher, explain what is happening and ask whether the homework can be adjusted.

## When it is too little

If your child seems to finish quickly and want more, you can encourage extra reading, projects or interests. Tutoring may suit a child who wants stretch. See [tutoring for a high-achieving child](/guides/tutoring-for-a-high-achieving-child).

## If homework is a battle

Check whether the work is too hard, whether your child is tired and how the routine works. See [how to motivate a reluctant learner](/guides/how-to-motivate-a-reluctant-learner).

## Rest matters

Children need play, sleep and family time too. Balance is important.
