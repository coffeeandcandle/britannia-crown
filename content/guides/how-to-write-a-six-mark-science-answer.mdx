Six-mark questions in GCSE science are **extended answers**. They are marked on how well an explanation hangs together, not on a checklist of facts.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Spend **a minute planning** before writing.
- Use **key scientific terms** and link each idea to the next with "because" or "so".
- Answer the **exact question** asked, such as "compare", "evaluate" or "describe".

</Callout>

## A simple method

1. **Read the command word.** "Describe" means say what happens, "explain" means say why, "compare" means point out similarities and differences, and "evaluate" means weigh up and conclude.
2. **Jot three or four points** in the margin in a sensible order.
3. **Write in short, linked sentences**, using terms from the course.
4. **Finish with a conclusion** if the question asks you to evaluate or decide.

## What top-level answers do

- Use accurate, specific terms rather than everyday words.
- Give a logical sequence, not a list of unrelated facts.
- Link cause and effect throughout.
- Stay on the question and avoid padding.
