Parents are often surprised that there is no single price for a private tutor in the UK. Hourly rates vary widely, and the cheapest option is not always the best value. This guide explains what drives the price, what to compare and how to avoid paying for things you do not need.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **Rates vary** by subject, level, the tutor's experience and qualifications, whether lessons are online or in person, and where you live.
- **Compare the whole package**, not just the hourly rate: matching, vetting, oversight and what happens if a tutor leaves.
- **Regular lessons usually cost less per hour** than booking ad hoc, and a monthly plan makes the cost predictable.

</Callout>

## What affects the price of a tutor

**Experience and qualifications.** A newly qualified teacher, a university student, a retired teacher and a specialist who marks exams for a board are all "tutors", but they charge differently and offer different things. Exam-board experience is especially valuable for GCSE and A-Level.

**Subject and level.** A-Level maths or chemistry usually costs more than primary tuition, because there are fewer people qualified to teach it well.

**Online or in person.** Online lessons remove travel time and widen your choice of tutors, so they are often better value. Face-to-face tuition adds travel and is limited by where you live. Our guide to [online versus face-to-face tutoring](/guides/online-vs-face-to-face-tutoring) covers the trade-offs.

**How the tutor is found.** A tutor you find yourself may charge less per hour, but you carry the risk: checking qualifications, safeguarding, references and finding a replacement if they move on. Agencies and consultancies charge for that work.

## What to compare, besides the hourly rate

When you collect quotes, ask the same questions each time:

- Is the tutor UK-qualified, and can you see evidence of it?
- Who checked their identity and safeguarding history?
- Is the tutor chosen for your child, or simply the next person available?
- Who oversees progress, and how often will you hear about it?
- What happens if the tutor becomes unavailable mid-term?
- Are there joining fees, minimum terms, or charges for cancelling lessons?

## Ways to keep the cost sensible

1. **Be specific about the goal.** "Raise a grade 5 to a grade 6 in maths by June" is easier to plan, and price, than "help with maths".
2. **Start with a trial or consultation** so you are not committed to a block of lessons with the wrong tutor.
3. **Use lessons for what a tutor does best:** explaining difficult ideas and exam technique, not supervising homework.
4. **Review after a half-term.** Ask for evidence of progress, and adjust hours up or down.

## Is a tutor worth it?

A good tutor is worth it when they address a specific gap, build confidence and give your child a regular routine. A poor match is expensive at any price. That is why matching and oversight matter more than shaving a few pounds off the hourly rate.
