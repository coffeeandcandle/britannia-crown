The move to secondary school is a big change. With a bit of preparation, most children settle well.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Expect **mixed feelings**: excitement and worry are both normal.
- Practise **practical routines** before September.
- Stay in touch with the school during the first term.

</Callout>

## What changes

Children go from one class and teacher to many teachers, rooms and subjects. They have more independence, more homework and a larger community. They may also be the youngest again.

## Before the move

- Go to **transition days** and open days.
- Talk about worries and let your child ask questions.
- Visit the school website together and look at the timetable.
- Practise the **journey**, and agree backup plans.
- Buy uniform and equipment early, with labels on everything.

## Building independence

Encourage your child to pack their own bag, manage a planner and keep track of homework. Start this over the summer.

## Friendships

Friendships can shift in Year 7. Encourage your child to join clubs, talk to new people and keep up existing friendships. Reassure them it is common to take time to settle.

## Learning

Gaps from primary may show up, particularly in maths and English. If your child struggles, speak to teachers early. A tutor can help rebuild skills and confidence. See [KS2 SATs explained](/guides/ks2-sats-explained).

## When to worry

If your child is persistently anxious, avoiding school or unhappy, talk to the form tutor or head of year. Early help works best.

## For parents

Keep communication open. Ask about the best and hardest parts of the day, listen, and give routines time to settle.
