Every year, families from abroad apply for UK schools. The rules depend on immigration status and the type of school, so it pays to plan early.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **Independent schools** often admit international pupils and help with visas.
- **State schools** have specific rules linked to immigration status.
- Plan **well ahead** for visas, guardians and entrance tests.

</Callout>

## Independent schools

Many independent schools welcome international pupils, especially boarders. They usually run their own admission process, including assessments and English language tests. Schools can sponsor student visas for eligible children, but conditions apply. See [boarding schools in the UK](/guides/boarding-schools-in-the-uk).

## State schools

State schools are for children who live in the UK. Eligibility depends on immigration status, so check the guidance on gov.uk and contact the local council. Children who move with their parents on certain visas may be able to attend.

## Guardianship

Schools commonly ask for a UK-based guardian who can act in an emergency and look after pupils during exeats and holidays. Agencies provide guardianship services, but check credentials.

## Language support

If English is an additional language, ask about support. See [children with English as an additional language](/guides/children-with-english-as-an-additional-language).

## Timeline

Start at least a year ahead. Schools often have registration deadlines, assessments and interviews. Allow time for visa processing.

## Costs

Independent fees can be significant. Ask for total costs, including fees, boarding, extras and guardianship.

## Getting advice

Rules change, so check official sources. A consultant can help you compare schools and plan the process.
