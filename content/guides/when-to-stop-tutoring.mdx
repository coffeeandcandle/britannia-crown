Tutoring is usually meant to be temporary. Knowing when it has done its job, or when it is no longer helping, saves money and avoids dependence.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Stop or reduce when the **original goal has been met** and your child is working independently.
- Consider **changing** if progress stalls or the fit is wrong.
- **Wind down gradually** and keep the door open.

</Callout>

## Signs the job is done

- Your child feels **confident** in the subject.
- They complete homework without help.
- Grades or class feedback match the goal you set.
- The tutor has **little new to teach** and sessions are mostly checking.

## Signs it is not working

- No change in confidence or understanding after a fair period.
- The tutor is **often late or unprepared** or lessons are repetitive.
- Your child is unhappy or anxious about sessions.
- You are not getting useful feedback.

See [how to know if tutoring is working](/guides/how-to-know-if-tutoring-is-working) for what to check first.

## Pausing for a while

Sometimes a break is better than ending. Exams, illness, holidays or a busy term might be good reasons to pause, with a plan to resume.

## Winding down well

- Agree with the tutor how many more lessons you will have.
- Reduce frequency gradually if your child is not ready to stop altogether.
- Ask for a **summary** of what has been covered and what to practise.
- Tell your child what they have achieved.

## Keeping the door open

Needs change. Your child might need help again at GCSE options, before exams, or with a new subject. A good tutor will welcome you back.
