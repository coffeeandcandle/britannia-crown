Dubai has many private schools following the English national curriculum. They attract British families because the system feels familiar, but there are differences worth knowing.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- British schools in Dubai follow the **English curriculum and exams**, with local additions.
- Schools are **regulated and rated** locally.
- **Admissions and waiting lists** can be competitive.

</Callout>

## What they teach

Most British schools in Dubai follow the Early Years Foundation Stage, then the national curriculum in primary and lower secondary, followed by GCSEs or IGCSEs and A-Levels. Some offer other qualifications as well.

## Differences from England

- **Arabic and local subjects** are taught in line with local requirements.
- **Fees** are charged, and vary widely.
- **Age cut-offs** and entry points may differ, so check each school.
- Many pupils are from a mix of nationalities, so the community is international.

## Regulation and ratings

Private schools in Dubai are regulated and inspected by the local education authority, which publishes ratings. See [KHDA ratings explained](/guides/khda-ratings-explained).

## Exams

Schools choose their boards, and may offer GCSEs, IGCSEs or both. If you plan to return to the UK, ask how your child's qualifications are viewed by UK sixth forms and universities. See [IGCSE vs GCSE](/guides/igcse-vs-gcse).

## Choosing a school

Visit, if possible, or take a virtual tour. Ask about class sizes, staff turnover, support for pupils with additional needs and results. Check the fee structure carefully.

## Timing

Places at popular schools can be limited and waiting lists are common. See [Dubai school admissions timeline](/guides/dubai-school-admissions-timeline).
