English Literature is a closed-book exam, so revision needs to build knowledge and the ability to write about it. A clear approach makes the workload manageable.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Know the **plot, characters, themes and context** of each text.
- Learn **short, flexible quotations**.
- Practise **planning and writing timed essays**.

</Callout>

## Know the texts

Make short summaries for each text: what happens, who the key characters are, the main themes and the context. A one-page summary per text, plus a list of important scenes, is a great base.

## Learn quotations

Choose a small set of quotations for each theme or character, with notes on key words and techniques. Short quotations are easier to remember and can often be used for several questions. Test yourself by covering the quote and writing it from memory.

## Understand context

Exam questions reward link between text and context, such as the time, place and ideas that shaped the writer. Keep context brief and relevant, rather than adding facts for their own sake.

## Poetry

Revise each poem's subject, mood, language and structure. Then practise comparing poems on themes. If the paper includes unseen poetry, practise reading and annotating new poems regularly.

## Practise essays

Write plans as well as full essays: a short plan, with thesis and key points, is a skill in itself. Practise timed answers, then check against the mark scheme or ask a teacher or tutor for feedback.

## Common mistakes

- Retelling the story instead of analysing.
- Using long quotations without explaining them.
- Not answering the exact question.
