Predicted grades are a school's estimate of the grades a student is likely to achieve. They matter because sixth forms, colleges and universities use them when making offers.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- A prediction is a **teacher's estimate**, not a guarantee.
- Predictions are based on **work, assessments and mocks**.
- Offers are often made on predictions, so **effort in Year 12** matters.

</Callout>

## Where predictions are used

- **Sixth form and college applications** use predicted GCSE grades.
- **UCAS applications** include predicted A-Level grades from the school.

## How they are set

Schools usually look at a mix of evidence: classwork, homework, assessments and mock exams. Policy differs, so ask the school how it sets predictions and whether it is cautious or optimistic.

## What predictions do and do not mean

A prediction is a sensible estimate of likely performance. It does not limit the student. Many students get higher or lower results, depending on how revision goes.

## What universities do with them

Universities consider predicted grades alongside the personal statement, subjects chosen and sometimes admissions tests or interviews. Offers often depend on meeting a set of grades.

## If a prediction seems low

- Ask the teacher what evidence was used and what would help raise it.
- Work on **mock results and assessments**, which show current performance.
- A polite, evidence-based conversation tends to help more than a complaint.

Schools make the final decision, but a student who improves their work during the year can often see predictions change.

## Setting realistic UCAS choices

Predictions help students choose a sensible mix of offers: some ambitious, some realistic and one safe. A consultant or careers adviser can help. See [A-Level grades and UCAS points](/guides/a-level-grades-and-ucas-points-explained).
