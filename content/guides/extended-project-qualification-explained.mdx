The EPQ is a project qualification some sixth form students take alongside A-Levels.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Students **choose a topic**, research it and produce a project.
- It can **strengthen university applications**.
- It needs **independent time management**.

</Callout>

## What it involves

Students plan, research and complete a project, which may be a long essay, an investigation or an artefact. They also reflect on the process and present their work. A supervisor at school guides them.

## Why students take it

- To explore an interest in depth.
- To build research and writing skills for university.
- To have something specific to discuss in personal statements and interviews.

## How universities see it

Many universities value the skills, and some make lower alternative offers for students who complete it. Others treat it as a bonus. Check policies for each course.

## Planning

- Choose a topic that the student finds interesting and that can be researched.
- Break the work into stages with deadlines.
- Keep a log of research and thinking.
- Allow time for drafting and review.

## Workload

It can take a lot of independent time over several months, alongside A-Level study. Students should consider whether they have the capacity.

## How parents can help

Ask about the project, encourage planning and offer feedback on drafts if invited. Do not do the work for them. See [UCAS personal statement guide](/guides/ucas-personal-statement-guide-for-parents).
