Online tutoring works well when the set-up is right. A few simple preparations can make the difference between a distracted session and a productive one.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Use a **quiet, tidy space** with a reliable internet connection.
- Test the **device, camera and sound** before the first lesson.
- Have **paper, pens and school materials** ready.

</Callout>

## The space

Choose a table or desk in a quiet room, away from television, siblings and loud noise. A chair that suits your child's height helps them sit comfortably. Natural light behind the screen, not behind your child, lets the tutor see them clearly.

## The device

A laptop or tablet with a good-sized screen is best. Check that the camera, microphone and speakers work. A charger nearby avoids battery trouble mid-lesson. For maths and sciences, a stylus or a pad of paper held up to the camera allows working to be shown.

## Internet

A wired or strong Wi-Fi connection avoids freezing and dropouts. If other people are streaming or downloading at the same time, the lesson can suffer. Close unused tabs and apps to keep the device running smoothly.

## Materials

Gather what your child will need: school exercise books, textbook or revision guide, past papers, calculator, ruler, coloured pens. Have these to hand before the lesson, so no time is lost.

## Reducing distractions

- Turn off notifications and close other apps and games.
- Put phones out of reach unless needed for the lesson.
- Agree how to handle interruptions, such as family members or deliveries.

## Etiquette for the lesson

Ask your child to be ready a few minutes early, with a drink of water. They should look at the screen, speak clearly and say if they have not understood something. Encourage them to type or write while they talk, so the tutor can see their thinking.

## Safeguarding

Sit nearby for younger children, keep lessons in a shared area of the home and ask your tutor or agency how they handle online safety. See [safeguarding questions to ask a tutor](/guides/safeguarding-questions-to-ask-a-tutor).
