The Early Years Foundation Stage, or EYFS, sets standards for learning, development and care from birth to age five in England.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- The EYFS covers **birth to the end of Reception**.
- It has **seven areas of learning**.
- Children are assessed at the end of Reception against **Early Learning Goals**.

</Callout>

## Who follows it

Nurseries, pre-schools, childminders and Reception classes in England follow the EYFS framework. Independent settings do too, with some exemptions for particular schools.

## The seven areas

Three **prime areas** come first:

- Communication and language.
- Personal, social and emotional development.
- Physical development.

Four **specific areas** build on them:

- Literacy.
- Mathematics.
- Understanding the world.
- Expressive arts and design.

## Learning through play

Young children learn best through play and exploration, with adults who talk to them, read with them and introduce new ideas. Good settings plan activities with learning goals, and watch children closely.

## Assessment

Practitioners observe children and share progress. In Reception, a Baseline Assessment takes place early on, and at the end of the year teachers assess each child against Early Learning Goals. See [reception baseline assessment explained](/guides/reception-baseline-assessment-explained).

## Supporting at home

- Talk, sing and read together every day.
- Count things and notice shapes.
- Let children play, build and make.
- Encourage independence, such as dressing and tidying.

## Choosing a setting

Ask how a setting uses the EYFS and how staff support each child's development. See [how to choose a nursery in England](/guides/how-to-choose-a-nursery-england).
