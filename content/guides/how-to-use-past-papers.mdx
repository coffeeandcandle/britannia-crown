Past papers are one of the best revision tools, because they show exactly what exams look like. They work best when used in a planned way.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Use papers from **the right exam board and specification**.
- Mix **untimed learning** with **timed practice**.
- The real learning is in **marking and fixing mistakes**.

</Callout>

## Find the right papers

Exam boards publish past papers, mark schemes and examiner reports on their websites. Choose papers for the correct qualification, tier and board. Older papers from a previous specification may differ.

## When to start

Early in revision, use questions by topic to build understanding. As exams approach, move to full papers to practise stamina and timing.

## How to work through a paper

1. Sit somewhere quiet, with the right equipment.
2. Time the paper, at least some of the time.
3. Avoid notes unless the paper allows them.
4. Mark carefully using the mark scheme.

## Learn from marking

The mark scheme shows how marks are awarded. Compare answers, note where marks were lost and write down what to do differently. Examiner reports explain common mistakes.

## Keep an error log

A short list of errors, with the correct method or fact, helps focus revision. Review it before the next paper.

## Avoid common traps

- Memorising answers instead of understanding.
- Doing many papers without checking mistakes.
- Using up all papers too early, so none are left for later.

## Judging progress

Compare marks across papers and watch trends. See [how GCSE grade boundaries work](/guides/how-gcse-grade-boundaries-work) before reading too much into a mark.
