If a result is much lower than expected, there are ways to ask for a check. Knowing how the process works helps you decide whether it is worth doing.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Ask the school **quickly**, as deadlines are short.
- A review checks the marking; it can **lower**, raise or leave the mark.
- Gather **evidence** such as predicted grades and mock results.

</Callout>

## Services after results

After results come out, exam boards offer services such as:

- **Access to scripts:** seeing the marked paper.
- **Review of marking:** checking that the mark scheme was applied properly.
- **Appeals:** a further step if the review does not resolve concerns.

The names and rules are set by the exam boards and by the regulator Ofqual, and can change, so check the current process with the school.

## Deciding whether to ask

Consider:

- **How far** the result is from what was expected.
- How close the mark is to a **grade boundary**. A paper just under a boundary is a better candidate than one far below.
- Whether the grade will affect a **place** at sixth form or university.

## The risk

A review can lower a grade as well as raise it. Discuss the risk with the school before asking, and ask what evidence suggests the mark may be wrong.

## Timing

Deadlines can be a matter of days. If a place depends on the result, tell the sixth form or university straight away. They may hold a place while the review happens.

## Other options

If the result stands, look at alternatives such as Clearing, a different course or a resit. See [results day: what to do](/guides/results-day-gcse-a-level-what-to-do) and [retaking A-Levels](/guides/retaking-a-levels).
