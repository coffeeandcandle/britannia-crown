Children in Years 1 and 2 are assessed in a number of ways. Understanding which are required helps put results in perspective.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Year 1 has a **phonics screening check**.
- Year 2 relies mostly on **teacher assessment**.
- Formal KS1 tests are **not compulsory** for schools.

</Callout>

## Year 1

The phonics screening check is a statutory check in June. See [the Year 1 phonics screening check](/guides/year-1-phonics-screening-check-explained). Teachers also keep their own records of children's progress.

## Year 2

At the end of Key Stage 1, teachers make assessments in reading, writing, maths and science. Schools may use past test papers to inform their judgement, but they are not required to administer KS1 tests. Check with your child's school what they do.

## What teachers look for

For English and maths, teachers judge whether children are working towards, at or above the expected standard for their age, using evidence from everyday work.

## How you are told

You should receive an annual report, and there are usually parents' evenings. Ask how the school describes progress, and what you can do at home.

## What matters at this age

The foundations of reading, number and writing, along with confidence and enjoyment, matter most. Do not worry about a single label. See [how to help your child read at home](/guides/how-to-help-your-child-read-at-home).

## Later assessments

Year 4 children take a multiplication tables check and Year 6 take SATs. See [KS2 SATs explained](/guides/ks2-sats-explained).
