Funded childcare can reduce nursery costs significantly, but the rules are detailed and have been changing. Always check the latest rules on gov.uk.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Funded hours are **government-funded** childcare hours for eligible children.
- Eligibility depends on **age, and often work and income**.
- **Apply early** and ask what extras are charged.

</Callout>

## Types of funding

England has several schemes, including a universal entitlement for three- and four-year-olds, and extra hours for eligible working parents. In recent years, the government has been extending funded hours to younger children, including some from the age of nine months for working parents. Other support exists for some two-year-olds. The details, thresholds and start dates have changed, so check current rules.

## How the hours work

Funded hours are usually spread across term time, or stretched across more weeks at fewer hours a week. A provider can explain how it offers them.

## Applying

Eligible families apply online and receive a code to share with their provider. Providers then claim funding from the local authority. Apply in good time, since codes need to be renewed periodically.

## What might still cost

- Meals, snacks and nappies.
- Additional hours beyond the funded amount.
- Trips and activities.
- Registration fees, depending on the setting.

Ask for a written breakdown of what is included and what is extra.

## Choosing a provider

Funded hours can be used with many nurseries, pre-schools and childminders. Quality varies, so visit and ask questions. See [how to choose a nursery in England](/guides/how-to-choose-a-nursery-england).

## Moving to school

Funded hours end when a child starts school. See [starting Reception](/guides/starting-reception-what-to-expect) for the transition.
