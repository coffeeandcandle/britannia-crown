US grades and UK year groups line up roughly, but not exactly. As a rule of thumb, **a US grade number is one lower than the UK year**, because UK children start school a year earlier.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- US **Kindergarten** is roughly **Year 1** (age 5 to 6); US **8th grade** is roughly **Year 9**.
- Schools in England place children by **date of birth**, not by the grade they were in.
- Cut-off dates differ, so placement can vary by a year.

</Callout>

## Rough equivalents

| US grade | Approximate age | England year group |
|---|---|---|
| Kindergarten | 5 to 6 | Year 1 |
| 1st grade | 6 to 7 | Year 2 |
| 5th grade | 10 to 11 | Year 6 |
| 6th grade | 11 to 12 | Year 7 |
| 7th grade | 12 to 13 | Year 8 |
| 8th grade | 13 to 14 | Year 9 |
| 9th grade (freshman) | 14 to 15 | Year 10 |
| 12th grade (senior) | 17 to 18 | Year 13 |

## Why placement can differ

England uses a 1 September cut-off, so a child's year is set by their birth date within that school year. Many US states use different cut-offs. Admissions teams may follow the age rule or consider the child's circumstances, so ask each school how it decides.

## Subjects and exams

UK secondary pupils sit GCSEs at the end of Year 11 and A-Levels at the end of Year 13. A child moving in Year 10 or 11 may be partway through exam courses, which can be hard to join. See [in-year admissions](/guides/in-year-school-admissions-moving-mid-year) and [international pupils applying to UK schools](/guides/international-pupils-applying-to-uk-schools).
