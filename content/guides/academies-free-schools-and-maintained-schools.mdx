England has several types of state school. The labels can be confusing, but the differences matter less to families than the quality and fit of the school.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **Maintained schools** are overseen by the local authority.
- **Academies and free schools** are state funded but run by trusts.
- **Independent schools** charge fees and are not state funded.

</Callout>

## Maintained schools

Community, foundation and voluntary schools are funded through the local authority. Some are faith schools. The local authority often handles admissions for community schools.

## Academies

Academies are publicly funded schools run by academy trusts. They are independent of local authority control, with more freedom over matters such as the curriculum and school day. Many secondary schools are now academies, and many belong to multi-academy trusts.

## Free schools

Free schools are a type of academy set up by groups such as parents, charities or trusts. They are funded in the same way as academies. They include some faith, specialist and new-model schools.

## Selective schools

Grammar schools are selective state schools. See [the 11-plus and grammar schools explained](/guides/11-plus-and-grammar-schools-explained).

## Independent schools

Independent schools charge fees and set their own admission rules. See [independent school entry](/guides/independent-school-entry-assessments-and-bursaries).

## Does the type matter?

Not as much as the individual school. Look at the school's culture, leadership, results and support. Judge each school on its own merits.

## Admissions

Academies and many other schools set their own admission policies, but they must follow the national admissions code. You still apply through the local authority in most cases, although some require a separate form.
