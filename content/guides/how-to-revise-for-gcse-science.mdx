GCSE science mixes facts, understanding, calculations and practical skills. A planned approach helps students cover it all.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Use **retrieval practice**, such as flashcards and quizzes.
- Practise **calculations and graphs**.
- Learn the **required practicals** and key command words.

</Callout>

## Know the specification

Check whether your child is taking combined or separate sciences, and which exam board. The specification lists everything that can be asked. See [GCSE science: combined or separate](/guides/gcse-science-combined-or-separate).

## Build knowledge

Make short notes or mind maps for each topic. Then test yourself: cover the notes and write what you remember. Repeat over days and weeks to strengthen memory.

## Diagrams and models

Draw and label diagrams, such as cells, circuits or reaction profiles. Explain processes aloud.

## Calculations and equations

Know which equations are given and which must be learned. Practise rearranging, using units and showing working.

## Required practicals

Revise the method, variables, risks and how to analyse results. Exam questions often ask students to evaluate or suggest improvements.

## Exam questions

- Learn command words such as describe, explain and evaluate.
- Practise extended answers using the mark scheme.
- Use past papers under timed conditions. See [how to use past papers](/guides/how-to-use-past-papers).

## Pace

Plan time across all three sciences. See [how to revise for GCSEs and A-Levels](/guides/how-to-revise-gcse-a-level).
