In the UK, "homeschooling" and "home education" mean the same thing: **educating a child at home instead of at school**. "Home education" is the more common term in official guidance.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- The terms are used **interchangeably**.
- In England, parents are **legally responsible** for providing a suitable education.
- You must **deregister** a child from school before home educating.

</Callout>

## Why the term differs

"Homeschooling" is widely used in the US and often suggests recreating school at home. "Home education" is used by UK families and officials and covers a wide range of approaches, from structured to child-led.

## What the law says

In England, education is compulsory but school attendance is not. Parents must make sure a child receives a full-time education suitable to their age, ability and any special needs. See [legal requirements](/guides/home-education-legal-requirements-england).

## Getting started

If your child is at school, you tell the school in writing that you are deregistering. See [deregistering your child from school](/guides/deregistering-your-child-from-school) and [how to start home education](/guides/how-to-start-home-education-england).

## Local authority

Local authorities may make informal enquiries but cannot generally require a home visit. See [home education and local authority contact](/guides/home-education-and-local-authority-contact) and our [home education guide](/guides/home-education-england).
