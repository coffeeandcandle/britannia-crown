Most students take **three A-Levels** over Years 12 and 13. Some take four, and some combine A-Levels with an EPQ or another qualification.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **Three A-Levels** is the standard choice for university applications.
- A **fourth subject**, often Further Maths, is possible but increases the workload.
- The **Extended Project Qualification** is a popular extra alongside three A-Levels.

</Callout>

## Three is the norm

University offers are usually made on three A-Levels, for example "AAB". Taking more rarely changes the offer, and the time may be better spent on the three that count.

## When four makes sense

Some students, especially those aiming for highly selective courses, take four. Further Maths is the common choice, and maths A-Level students who also take Further Maths often count it as a separate subject. Check how your sixth form handles it.

## The EPQ

The [Extended Project Qualification](/guides/extended-project-qualification-explained) is an independent research project taken alongside A-Levels. Some universities make lower offers for students with an EPQ, but this varies.

## Mixing qualifications

Some students take A-Levels with a BTEC or choose a different route entirely. See [BTEC vs A-Level](/guides/btec-vs-a-level) and [T-Levels explained](/guides/t-levels-explained).

## Choosing well

Pick subjects your child enjoys and is good at, and check degree requirements early. Our [A-Level subject choices guide](/guides/a-level-subject-choices-and-university) explains how, and our [tutor page for A-Level Maths](/tutors/a-level-maths) covers support.
