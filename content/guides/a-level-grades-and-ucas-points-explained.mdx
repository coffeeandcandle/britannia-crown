A-Level results are graded A* to E. Universities state offers either as grades, such as AAB, or as UCAS points. This guide explains how both work.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- A-Levels are graded **A* to E**, with U for ungraded.
- UCAS points: **A* 56, A 48, B 40, C 32, D 24, E 16**.
- Many offers are in **grades**, not points. Check each one carefully.

</Callout>

## Grades

An A* is the top grade, followed by A, B, C, D and E. Below E is U, which means unclassified. Most universities want C or better, and competitive courses ask for As and A*s.

## The UCAS tariff

UCAS gives points to qualifications to help universities compare applicants. For a full A-Level, the points are:

| Grade | UCAS points |
| --- | --- |
| A* | 56 |
| A | 48 |
| B | 40 |
| C | 32 |
| D | 24 |
| E | 16 |

Some universities state offers in points, such as 120 points, which could be reached in different ways, for example BBB or ABC with other qualifications. Check whether the university counts all your qualifications.

## Offers in grades

Many offers are stated as grades, for example AAB. This means one A and two Bs in three A-Levels, often with a requirement in particular subjects. A grade offer is more specific than a points offer.

## Conditional and unconditional offers

A **conditional offer** depends on results. If your child meets the conditions, they get a place. Some universities make offers that depend on other things too, such as an interview, admissions test or portfolio.

## AS-Levels

AS-Levels are now generally separate qualifications and do not count towards the final A-Level grade. See [A-Level subject choices and university requirements](/guides/a-level-subject-choices-and-university).

## Other qualifications

BTECs, T Levels and the Extended Project Qualification have their own tariff points. See [BTEC vs A-Level](/guides/btec-vs-a-level) and [T Levels explained](/guides/t-levels-explained).
