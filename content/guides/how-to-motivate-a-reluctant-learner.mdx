Almost every parent meets a child who says "I don't want to" or "what's the point?". Reluctance is usually a sign of something underneath. Finding it is the first step to helping.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Look for the **reason behind** the reluctance before trying to fix it.
- Use **small goals, choice and praise for effort**.
- If it comes with distress, **seek help** from school or your GP.

</Callout>

## Find the cause

Ask your child, calmly and without blame, what they find hard or boring. Check with the teacher. Common causes include:

- work that is **too hard**, which makes them feel stupid;
- work that is **too easy**, which makes it dull;
- a **gap in understanding** that has grown over time;
- worries about friends, bullying or home;
- tiredness, hunger or too many activities.

## Make goals small and visible

"Do well in maths" is too big. "Finish these five questions" is something a child can see and tick off. Small wins build the belief that effort works.

## Give some control

Let your child choose when to start homework, which task to do first or how to revise. Choice gives a sense of ownership, which often reduces resistance.

## Connect work to their world

Link learning to what they care about: football statistics for maths, a favourite author for English, cooking for fractions and science. Interest is a powerful motivator.

## Praise effort, not ability

"You worked really hard on that" encourages persistence. "You're so clever" can make children afraid to try something they might fail at.

## Keep routines gentle

A regular time and place for work helps, but do not let it become a nightly battle. Short sessions, breaks and an end point make it easier to begin.

## When a tutor can help

Sometimes a child responds better to someone who is not their parent. A patient tutor can rebuild confidence and make learning feel manageable again. See [tutoring for a child who is behind](/guides/tutoring-for-a-child-who-is-behind).

<Cta title="Struggling to get your child engaged?">

Talk to an education consultant about what might help.

</Cta>
