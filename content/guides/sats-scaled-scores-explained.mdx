KS2 SATs results are reported as **scaled scores**, not grades or percentages. A score of **100 means a child has met the expected standard**.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Scaled scores run from **80 to 120**.
- **100 or more** means the expected standard was met.
- The conversion changes each year so that results are comparable.

</Callout>

## How raw marks become scaled scores

A child's raw mark, the number of questions correct, is converted onto the scaled score using a table published each year. Because tests vary in difficulty, the same raw mark can give slightly different scores in different years.

## What the numbers mean

| Scaled score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 100 or above | Expected standard met |
| Below 100 | Expected standard not yet met |
| Around 110 or above | Working well above the standard |

Scores below the bottom of the scale are reported differently. Your child's school will explain exactly how results are shown.

## Teacher assessments

Writing is assessed by teachers, and reported as working at or towards the expected standard. Read more in [KS2 SATs explained](/guides/ks2-sats-explained).

## What to do with the result

Use it as a snapshot, not a verdict. Secondary schools may use it to set groups in Year 7, so share the result with the new school. If it points to gaps, [maths at home](/guides/how-to-help-your-child-with-maths-at-home) is a good starting point, and our [Year 6 to Year 7 guide](/guides/moving-from-year-6-to-year-7) covers the transition.
