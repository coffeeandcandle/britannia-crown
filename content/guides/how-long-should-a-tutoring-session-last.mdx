Session length affects both how much your child learns and how they feel about tutoring. Too short and little gets done. Too long and attention fades. This guide explains typical lengths by age and how to judge what works for your child.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **Younger children** usually do best with 30 to 45 minutes.
- **Secondary and sixth-form students** often manage 60 to 90 minutes.
- The right length is the one where your child is **still engaged at the end**.

</Callout>

## Typical lengths by age

**Early primary (ages 5 to 7).** Short sessions of around 30 minutes, with movement or a change of activity, tend to work best.

**Upper primary (ages 7 to 11).** Between 45 minutes and an hour is common, depending on concentration and how tiring the school day has been.

**Secondary (ages 11 to 16).** An hour is the usual starting point. Some students prefer 90 minutes for essay-based subjects or full exam questions.

**Sixth form.** One hour to 90 minutes, with longer slots for extended writing or problem sets.

## What good use of time looks like

A well-run session usually has a clear shape: a quick recap, new learning or practice, then a short check that it has stuck. If most of the lesson is spent waiting, searching for materials or chatting, the session is too long or poorly planned.

## Signs the session length is wrong

- Your child is **drifting or fidgeting** halfway through. Try a shorter session or a break.
- The tutor **never finishes** what they planned. A longer slot, or a narrower plan, may help.
- Your child is **exhausted afterwards** every week, especially if the lesson follows a full school day. Consider moving to a weekend or earlier slot.

## Timing matters as much as length

Late in the evening, many children have little left. Lessons straight after school work better for some, while others need a snack and a break first. Ask your child what suits them, and be open to trying a different time.

## Online sessions

Concentration online can fall more quickly, so some tutors shorten lessons or build in short activities away from the screen. See [online or face-to-face tutoring](/guides/online-vs-face-to-face-tutoring) for more.
