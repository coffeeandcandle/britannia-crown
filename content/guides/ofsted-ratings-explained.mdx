Ofsted inspects schools in England and publishes reports that many parents use when choosing a school. Reports are useful, but need to be read carefully.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Ofsted reports are **a snapshot** from a particular inspection.
- The **format has been changing**, so read the report itself, not just a label.
- Use reports **alongside visits and conversations**.

</Callout>

## What Ofsted does

Ofsted inspects state schools and reports on matters such as the quality of education, behaviour, personal development and leadership. Inspectors visit, observe lessons, look at pupils' work and talk to staff and pupils.

## How reporting has changed

For many years Ofsted gave schools an overall rating, such as Outstanding, Good, Requires Improvement or Inadequate. Ofsted has been replacing single-word overall judgments with report cards that grade several areas separately. Older reports still use the earlier labels, so check which format the school's latest report uses and when the inspection took place.

## How to read a report

- Check the **date**. A report from several years ago may no longer reflect the school.
- Look at the **detailed findings**, not only the headline.
- Notice what is said about **behaviour, teaching, support for pupils** and the curriculum.
- Read what inspectors say the school needs to improve.

## Limits of a report

Reports describe a few days. They do not tell you how happy your child will be, or how a school handles particular needs. Schools can also change quickly, for better or worse, with new leadership.

## Looking at the wider picture

- Visit the school and ask questions. See [questions to ask when visiting a school](/guides/questions-to-ask-when-visiting-a-school).
- Talk to current parents.
- Look at exam results and progress data. See [Progress 8 and Attainment 8 explained](/guides/progress-8-and-attainment-8-explained).
- Think about location, size, values and your child's needs.

## Where to find reports

Ofsted publishes reports on its website, searchable by school name. Independent schools are inspected by other bodies, such as the Independent Schools Inspectorate.
