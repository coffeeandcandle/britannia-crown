Home education is legal in England, and parents are responsible for making sure their child receives a suitable education. Understanding the legal basis helps you start with confidence.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Parents must ensure their child receives an **efficient, full-time, suitable education**.
- You can do this **at school or otherwise**, and do not need teaching qualifications.
- There is **no set curriculum or timetable**, but local authorities can ask questions.

</Callout>

## The legal duty

The law places the duty on parents to ensure that children of compulsory school age receive an efficient full-time education suitable to their age, ability and aptitude, and any special educational needs. They can do so by regular attendance at school or by other means.

## What this means in practice

- There is **no requirement** to follow the national curriculum.
- You do not need to teach specific hours or follow school terms.
- You do not need to be a qualified teacher.
- You may choose your own methods and resources.

## What counts as suitable

Suitable education prepares a child for life in modern society and enables them to achieve their potential. It is up to the parent to show that the education is suitable if asked.

## Local authorities

Local authorities have a duty to identify children not receiving a suitable education. They can make informal enquiries, but parents do not have to allow home visits or meet in person, though cooperating often helps. See [home education and local authority contact](/guides/home-education-and-local-authority-contact).

## Children with special needs or EHCPs

Children with an EHC plan naming a school, or attending a special school through local authority arrangements, may need extra steps. See [home education for children with SEN](/guides/home-education-for-children-with-sen).

## Rules may change

Proposals for registers of children not in school and other changes have been discussed. Check the latest position on gov.uk before you begin.
