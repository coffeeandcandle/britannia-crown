Socialising is one of the most common worries about home education. With planning, children can build lasting friendships and confidence.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Children can socialise through **groups, clubs and community activities**.
- Quality and variety matter more than quantity.
- Include **mixed ages**, not only children of the same age.

</Callout>

## Where children meet others

- **Home education groups and co-ops**, which often meet weekly.
- **Clubs:** sport, music, drama, art and coding.
- **Community groups** such as scouts, guides or youth clubs.
- **Libraries** and community events.
- **Classes** at local colleges or museums.

## Building a routine

Plan a regular week with several social activities, along with quiet time at home. Many families find two or three fixed commitments work well.

## Friendships across ages

Home educated children often mix with children and adults of different ages. This can help confidence and communication, though some children may also want peers their own age.

## If your child is shy

Try small meetings first, and arrange playdates. Practise in a gentle setting, and let your child step back when needed.

## Teenagers

Older children often want independence and a peer group. Look at youth clubs, volunteering, college taster courses and online communities with safe settings. See [qualifications for home educated children](/guides/qualifications-for-home-educated-children).

## Parent support

Joining groups also helps parents. Sharing ideas and resources eases isolation.

## Reviewing

Ask your child how they feel about friendships, and adjust. If they are lonely, try new activities. See [home education in England](/guides/home-education-england).
