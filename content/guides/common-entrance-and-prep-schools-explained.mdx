For many families, the route to an independent senior school runs through a prep school. The system has its own terms and timetable.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Prep schools prepare children for **senior school entry**, often at 13.
- Entry processes include **pre-tests, Common Entrance, interviews and references**.
- Senior schools may ask for **early registration**.

</Callout>

## What prep schools do

They teach primary-age and early secondary-age children, often up to Year 8, and prepare them for senior schools. Some are day schools, some have boarding. Class sizes and facilities vary.

## Entry at 13

Senior schools often admit at 13, though some also admit at 11 or 14. Methods vary: pre-tests in Year 6 or 7, interviews, school references and sometimes Common Entrance exams in Year 8.

## Common Entrance

Common Entrance papers are used by some senior schools as part of entry. They cover subjects such as English, maths and science. Some schools use them to confirm places already offered conditionally, while others use other assessments instead.

## Pre-tests

Many schools use computer-based pre-tests at an earlier stage to assess ability. Dates and format depend on the schools you apply to.

## Planning

- Research senior schools early and check their registration deadlines.
- Ask prep schools which senior schools their pupils go to.
- Look at scholarship deadlines if relevant.
