The move from primary to secondary school is a big step. Children go from one class and one teacher to many teachers, new routines and a larger building. Most settle well, and a little preparation helps.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Use the **transition days** in the summer term and ask the school what it provides.
- Practise the **practical routines** before September: travel, timetable, equipment and homework.
- Watch for **gaps in English and maths**, which show up quickly in Year 7.

</Callout>

## Before the summer holidays

- Attend any **induction or transition days** the school offers.
- Ask for the **uniform, equipment and timetable** details early.
- Find out how the school passes information from primary, and tell it about anything that helps your child settle.

## Practical preparation

- **Travel.** Practise the journey, including what to do if a bus is missed.
- **Organisation.** A planner, a packed bag and a place for homework help.
- **Independence.** Lunch money or payment systems, lost property and asking a teacher for help.

## Emotional preparation

Worries are normal. Talk about friendships, getting lost and new teachers, and let your child lead the conversation. Reassure them that most pupils feel unsure in the first weeks.

## Academic preparation

Primary results do not always show the whole picture, so look at reading, writing and number fluency over the summer. See [KS2 SATs explained](/guides/ks2-sats-explained) for how results are reported. Gentle reading and maths practice is enough for most children. If you see gaps, early support at Key Stage 3 is easier than catching up later.

## The first term

Expect tiredness. Keep routines steady, check in about friendships and homework, and speak to the form tutor early if something worries you. If your child is struggling to settle, see [signs your child needs a tutor](/guides/signs-your-child-needs-a-tutor) and [how much homework is normal](/guides/homework-how-much-is-normal).
