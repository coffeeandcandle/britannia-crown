After GCSEs, young people in England must stay in education or training until 18. Choosing where is a big decision, and it helps to start early.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Options include **school sixth forms, sixth form colleges and further education colleges**.
- Check **entry requirements, courses and results**.
- Visit **open days** and apply early.

</Callout>

## The main options

**School sixth form.** Part of a secondary school, often with smaller classes and familiar surroundings.

**Sixth form college.** Specialises in 16 to 19 students, often with a wide range of A-Levels.

**Further education college.** Offers many courses, including vocational and technical routes alongside A-Levels.

**Apprenticeship or training.** Combines work with study.

## What to compare

- **Courses** offered and whether they match your child's goals.
- **Entry requirements** for each course.
- **Results and destinations**: what students go on to do.
- **Support**: pastoral care and help with applications.
- **Travel**: time, cost and practicalities.
- The **feel** of the place, which an open day shows.

## Entry requirements

Providers set their own. A common pattern is five GCSEs at grade 4 or above, including English and maths, with higher grades for specific subjects. See [how many GCSEs does a child need](/guides/how-many-gcses-does-a-child-need).

## Timeline

Open days often run in the autumn, with applications in the autumn and winter, and offers conditional on GCSE results. Check each provider's dates, and apply to more than one.

## Questions to ask on open days

- How many students achieve my grades, and what do they go on to do?
- What support is there with applications and careers?
- What is the timetable like, and how is independent study supported?
- Can students change courses early on?
