Whether tutoring is worth it depends on what you want it to do. It helps most when there is a **specific goal** and the tutor is a good fit.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Tutoring helps most when there is a **clear gap, a goal or an exam ahead**.
- Results depend on **fit, consistency and the child's engagement**.
- Set a goal and **review after a term**.

</Callout>

## When it helps

- A child has fallen behind in a topic or subject.
- Confidence has dropped.
- A key exam, such as the 11-plus, GCSEs or A-Levels, is approaching.
- A confident child wants more challenge. See [tutoring for a high-achieving child](/guides/tutoring-for-a-high-achieving-child).

## When it may not help

If a child is exhausted, or the problem is not academic, extra lessons may not be the answer. Speak to the school first. See [signs your child needs a tutor](/guides/signs-your-child-needs-a-tutor).

## Getting value

- Agree **specific goals**.
- Choose a tutor who fits your child's learning style.
- Keep to a **regular schedule**.
- **Review progress** every few weeks. See [how to know if tutoring is working](/guides/how-to-know-if-tutoring-is-working).

## Cost

Compare options on more than price. See [how much a private tutor costs](/guides/how-much-does-a-private-tutor-cost-uk) and [when to stop tutoring](/guides/when-to-stop-tutoring).
