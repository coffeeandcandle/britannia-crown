Deciding between an independent and a state school is partly about money and partly about what you want for your child. Looking at individual schools matters more than sector labels.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Both sectors include **excellent and weaker schools**.
- Independent schools charge **fees** and set their own admission rules.
- Look at **fit, support and progress**, not just reputation.

</Callout>

## What differs

**Fees.** Independent schools charge fees, and VAT now applies to most of them. Ask for a full breakdown of costs, including extras such as uniform, trips and music lessons.

**Admissions.** Independent schools set their own process, often with assessments, interviews and registration deadlines. See [independent school entry](/guides/independent-school-entry-assessments-and-bursaries).

**Curriculum.** Independent schools are not required to follow the national curriculum. Many do, and many offer GCSEs, IGCSEs and A-Levels or the IB.

**Class sizes and facilities.** Often smaller classes and more facilities, but this varies.

## What can be similar

Many state schools offer strong teaching, wide activities and good pastoral care. Selective grammar schools and high-performing comprehensives can match independent schools on results.

## Questions to ask

- What are the **total costs** over the years, and how often do fees rise?
- How do you support pupils who find learning hard, and those who excel?
- What **extras** are included?
- Where do pupils go after leaving?

## Bursaries and scholarships

Some schools offer financial help. Check eligibility early, as deadlines can be strict.

## Thinking long term

Consider whether you can sustain fees for several years, and how your child will cope with moving if circumstances change.

## Getting a balanced view

Visit both types of school, talk to families and ask your child what feels right. Consider a consultation to compare options.
