Catchment areas are one of the most misunderstood parts of school admissions. Understanding how they work helps you judge your chances honestly.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- A catchment area is a **zone around a school** that some schools use in admissions.
- Many schools use **distance** or other rules instead.
- Living in a catchment is **not a guarantee** of a place.

</Callout>

## What a catchment is

A catchment area is a defined geographical area that a school gives priority to when applications exceed places. The boundary is set by the school or local authority and published in the admission policy.

## Catchment or distance?

Many schools do not have catchments. They rank applicants by distance from the school, usually in a straight line. In some places, schools share a priority area. Each school's admission policy explains which rules apply.

## Why it is not a guarantee

If a school is popular, children from the catchment may still be ranked by other criteria, such as having a sibling at the school or living nearest. In some years, the furthest distance a place is offered can shrink, even within a catchment.

## Checking your chances

- Read the school's **admission policy** and your local authority's admissions booklet.
- Look for **last distance offered** or similar data for previous years.
- Ask the local authority how places were offered.
- Remember that data from past years is a guide, not a promise.

## Moving house

Moving to get into a catchment can be risky. Admission rules are based on your address on the closing date, and policies can change. Check policies carefully, and be cautious about renting short term just to gain an address. Local authorities check addresses and can withdraw a place if the information given was misleading.

## What to do if you are outside

Apply anyway to your preferred schools, and list realistic alternatives. See [what if you don't get your first choice school](/guides/what-if-you-dont-get-your-first-choice-school).
