Home educating several children of different ages needs organisation, but also brings advantages: children can learn from each other, and topics can be shared.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **Share topics**, but set tasks by age.
- Use **independent work** for older children.
- Give each child **one-to-one time**.

</Callout>

## Combine where you can

History, geography, science, art and read-aloud books suit mixed ages. Ask older children to dig deeper, while younger ones draw, discuss or build.

## Separate where you must

Maths and literacy usually need individual levels. Use workbooks or courses that suit each child, and set aside focused teaching time for each.

## Staggering and independence

While you teach one child, others can read, do independent practice or use educational activities. Build independence step by step.

## Older children helping younger

Older siblings can read to younger ones or explain a concept. This can help both, but do not rely on it too much.

## Routines

Rhythm helps: a morning meeting, core lessons, a break, then projects or activities. Keep it flexible, and adapt if a child needs more time.

## Exam years

Plan GCSE or IGCSE preparation in good time, with specific teaching and past papers. A tutor can help when you are stretched. See [qualifications for home educated children](/guides/qualifications-for-home-educated-children).

## Looking after yourself

Build in breaks, rest and support from other families. See [home education approaches](/guides/structured-or-unschooling-home-education-approaches).
