A good GCSE science tutor knows the **exam board**, the **route** your child takes and how marks are earned. These are the things to check.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Ask about **experience with your child's board** and with combined or triple science.
- Look for **clear explanations** and exam technique, not just content delivery.
- Check **safeguarding and qualifications** before the first lesson.

</Callout>

## What to look for

- **Subject knowledge** across biology, chemistry and physics, or a specialist if your child needs one.
- **Exam board knowledge**, including required practicals and question styles.
- **Maths confidence**, since science questions use calculations and graphs.
- **A clear plan** with goals and a way to track progress.

## Questions to ask

- Which boards and routes have you taught?
- How do you decide what to cover first?
- How will you track and report progress?
- What safeguarding checks have you had? See [safeguarding questions to ask a tutor](/guides/safeguarding-questions-to-ask-a-tutor).

Expect questions about recent work and goals, then a short example of how the tutor works. See [what to expect from a first tutoring lesson](/guides/what-to-expect-from-a-first-tutoring-lesson).
