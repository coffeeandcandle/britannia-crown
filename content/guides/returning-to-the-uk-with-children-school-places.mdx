Moving back to the UK after living abroad involves a lot of logistics, and your children's schooling is often the biggest worry. Places are not guaranteed, curricula may differ and timing is rarely ideal. This guide explains how to approach school places and what to prepare when you return to England.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- You usually **apply through your local authority** once you have a UK address, and places depend on **availability.**
- **Start early.** Contact the council and schools before you arrive if you can.
- Check how your child's **current curriculum and exams** compare with what the new school offers.

</Callout>

## When can you apply?

Councils generally need a **UK address** to process an application, so the date you secure accommodation often sets the timetable. Some local authorities accept applications ahead of a move when you can show evidence of a confirmed address, such as a tenancy or completion date, so ask. If you are returning at the start of a school year, be aware of the main admission deadlines in [school admissions in England](/guides/school-admissions-england).

## How the process works

If you are arriving outside the normal admissions round, your application is an **in-year application**. Our guide to [in-year admissions](/guides/in-year-school-admissions-moving-mid-year) explains how this works, including how to apply, how waiting lists operate and what to do if there is no space. In summary:

1. **Find the right council** for your new address and read its in-year admissions information.
2. **Apply to several schools** if you can, so you have alternatives.
3. **Prepare documents:** proof of address, your child's birth certificate or passport and previous school records.
4. **Ask about waiting lists** and appeals. See [admission appeals](/guides/school-admission-appeals-england).

## Differences to expect

**Curriculum and year groups.** Children's year groups and what they have covered may differ from England, especially if they have attended an international school or followed another system. Ask the new school how it will assess your child and settle them in.

**Exam boards and courses.** For secondary-age children, check which exam boards and subjects the new school offers. Moving mid-GCSE or A-Level can mean different specifications. International GCSEs and GCSEs are similar but not identical. Read [IGCSE and GCSE differences](/guides/igcse-vs-gcse).

**Language and culture.** Children may need time to adjust to routines, expectations and social life.

## Keeping learning going

A gap in schooling can happen during a move. Plan how your child will keep learning in the weeks before a place is confirmed, with reading, maths practice and, if helpful, tutoring. A matched tutor can bridge differences in content, build confidence and help with a smooth start.

## Visit and ask questions

If you can, visit or arrange a virtual tour. Our [list of questions for school visits](/guides/questions-to-ask-when-visiting-a-school) will help you compare schools quickly. Ask specifically how the school welcomes children arriving from abroad.

## Looking after wellbeing

Moves are emotional for children. Keep routines steady where possible, involve them in choices and be honest about what is happening. Talk to the school about how they will support your child's first weeks.
