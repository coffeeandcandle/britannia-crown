Tutoring is an investment of money and time, so it is fair to ask whether it is working. Progress is not always a higher mark straight away. This guide shows what to look for and when to act.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Look at **confidence, effort and understanding** as well as grades.
- Allow **a term or so** for results to show, longer for large gaps.
- Ask for **regular, specific feedback** from the tutor.

</Callout>

## Early signs

In the first few weeks, look for:

- your child talking about the subject more positively;
- **less panic** over homework, or more willingness to start it;
- your child being able to **explain** something they learned;
- more questions in class.

## Medium-term signs

After a term or so, you might see:

- better marks in class tests or homework;
- teachers noticing more participation;
- fewer gaps flagged in reports;
- your child moving up a set or tackling harder questions.

## Ask for specific feedback

A good tutor will tell you what has been covered, what your child finds difficult and what comes next. Vague comments such as "doing fine" are less useful. Agree how and when you will get updates, whether a short message after each lesson or a longer review each half term.

## Involve the school

Ask your child's teacher what they have noticed. Teachers know the curriculum and exam board, and can confirm whether the tutoring matches what is needed.

## If progress seems slow

Before deciding it is not working:

1. **Check expectations.** Is the goal realistic for the time available?
2. **Check consistency.** Missed lessons and little practice slow progress.
3. **Check the match.** Does your child like and trust the tutor?
4. **Ask the tutor** what they would change.

If the answers do not point to a fix, a different tutor or approach may be needed. See [when to stop tutoring](/guides/when-to-stop-tutoring).
