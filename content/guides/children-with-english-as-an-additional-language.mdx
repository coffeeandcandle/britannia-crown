Children who are new to English can do very well at UK schools, with patience and the right support.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Children often pick up **everyday English quickly**, but academic English takes longer.
- Keep using your **home language** too.
- Ask the school what **support** is available.

</Callout>

## What schools do

Schools support pupils learning English in different ways, such as extra help in class, small groups, bilingual resources and peer buddies. Ask what the school offers and how they track progress.

## Everyday and academic English

Children can chat in English within months, but the language of textbooks and exams, such as in science and history, takes longer. This is normal, and children benefit from continued support.

## What parents can do

- **Talk** in the language you are most comfortable with, and keep your home language alive.
- **Read** together in both languages.
- Encourage **talk about school**.
- Be patient, and celebrate small steps.

## Reading and writing

Reading daily is one of the best ways to build vocabulary. Use picture books, graded readers and audiobooks. Write short pieces such as diaries or lists. See [how to help your child read at home](/guides/how-to-help-your-child-read-at-home).

## Transitions

Moving school can be challenging for children still learning English. Tell the new school about your child's background, strengths and previous schooling. See [moving from primary to secondary school](/guides/moving-from-primary-to-secondary-school).

## Exams

Students may be able to take exams in English with support, but rules are specific. Speak to the school's exams officer.

## Tutoring

A tutor can offer one-to-one time to build vocabulary and confidence. See [private tutoring](/services/tutoring).
