Living abroad is a great opportunity, but families often worry about continuity of learning, particularly if they plan to return to the UK.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Choose a school whose **curriculum and exams** fit your plans.
- Use **tutoring** to fill gaps or maintain core skills.
- Keep **records** for a future move.

</Callout>

## Choosing the right curriculum

British-curriculum schools abroad make transfers back to the UK easier, but they vary in quality. Other curricula, such as the IB or American, are different, and may require bridging. Ask how exams and year groups map to the English system.

## Common gaps

Gaps often appear in English grammar, spelling, UK history and the specific content of GCSE courses. Maths topics can also be taught in a different order. A tutor can help identify and fill gaps.

## Exams

Many pupils abroad take IGCSEs, which are widely accepted in the UK. Check requirements for UK sixth forms and universities. See [IGCSE vs GCSE](/guides/igcse-vs-gcse).

## Tutoring from overseas

Online tutoring is widely used by expatriate families. Sessions fit around school hours and time zones. See [online or face-to-face tutoring](/guides/online-vs-face-to-face-tutoring).

## Planning a return

- Keep school reports, assessments and exam certificates.
- Research UK schools and their admission rules early. See [returning to the UK with children](/guides/returning-to-the-uk-with-children-school-places).
- Consider a trial period or summer catch-up.

## Looking after wellbeing

Moving can be unsettling. Keep routines, stay in touch with family and friends and talk about the move together.
