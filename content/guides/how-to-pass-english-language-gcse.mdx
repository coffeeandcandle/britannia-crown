To pass GCSE English Language, a student needs a **grade 4 or above**. Marks come from reading and writing in equal measure, so both need attention.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **Half the marks are for writing**, which is quick to improve with practice.
- Learn **what each question asks** and practise answering it in the time given.
- **Accuracy** in spelling, punctuation and grammar picks up easy marks.

</Callout>

## Where marks come from

Both papers combine reading questions and a writing task. See [the papers explained](/guides/gcse-english-language-papers-explained). A weak spot in one area can be balanced by strength in the other.

## What to practise

- **Short reading questions**: finding and quoting evidence quickly.
- **Analysis**: explaining how language and structure create effects.
- **Timed writing**: planning for a few minutes, then writing in paragraphs.
- **Proofreading**: leaving two or three minutes to check for errors.

## Common problems

- Retelling the text instead of analysing it.
- Running out of time on the final writing task.
- Repeating the same spelling and punctuation errors.

## If a grade 4 is missed

Students in England who do not reach a grade 4 are generally expected to keep studying English after 16. See [GCSE resits](/guides/gcse-resits-english-and-maths).
