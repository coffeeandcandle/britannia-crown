Maths is a subject you learn by doing. The best revision is mostly practising questions, checking mistakes and trying again.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **Do questions**, rather than just reading notes.
- Start with **weak topics**, then move to full timed papers.
- **Learn from mistakes** using mark schemes.

</Callout>

## Step 1: Find the gaps

Use a topic list from the specification, or a recent paper, to tick off what feels secure and what does not. Prioritise topics that carry a lot of marks and that your child finds hard.

## Step 2: Practise by topic

For each weak topic, watch or read a short explanation, then try a set of questions. Check answers against a mark scheme. If marks were lost, work out why: method, arithmetic or misreading.

## Step 3: Mix topics

Maths exams mix everything. Once topics feel stronger, practise mixed questions so your child has to decide what method to use.

## Step 4: Use past papers

Do whole papers under timed conditions, then mark them honestly. See [how to use past papers](/guides/how-to-use-past-papers).

## Step 5: Master the basics

Fractions, percentages, ratio, algebra and number skills appear across the papers. Short regular practice keeps them sharp, especially for the non-calculator paper.

## Exam technique

- Show working, since method marks matter.
- Read the question twice.
- Check units, signs and whether the answer is sensible.
- Move on if stuck, then return.

## Tiers

Students sitting Higher need confidence with harder questions, while Foundation students should aim to secure as many marks as possible on core topics. See [Foundation or Higher tier](/guides/foundation-or-higher-tier-gcse).
