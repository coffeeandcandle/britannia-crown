Home educated children can gain qualifications just like school pupils. Planning early makes the process far easier.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Children take exams as **private candidates** at an exam centre.
- **GCSEs and IGCSEs** are the most common routes.
- Start planning **at least a year ahead**.

</Callout>

## Why qualifications matter

Sixth forms, colleges, apprenticeships and employers often ask for GCSEs or equivalents. Not every child needs them, but having options is useful.

## GCSEs and IGCSEs

Both are widely accepted. IGCSEs are often assessed by final exams, with little or no coursework, which can suit private candidates. Some subjects with practical or speaking elements are hard to take privately. See [IGCSE vs GCSE](/guides/igcse-vs-gcse).

## Finding an exam centre

Not every school accepts private candidates. Contact local centres early, as many have fixed deadlines and fees. See [entering GCSEs as a private candidate](/guides/private-candidate-gcse-how-to-enter).

## Other qualifications

Alternatives include Functional Skills in English and maths, vocational awards, college courses from age 14 or 16 and Open University modules for older students. Check what the next step requires.

## Planning ahead

- Decide which subjects your child needs.
- Choose boards and specifications.
- Book exam entries early.
- Allow time for past papers and teaching.

## Support

Some families teach all subjects themselves, while others use tutors, online courses or classes for the harder ones. See [home education curriculum choices](/guides/home-education-curriculum-choices).

## Costs

Exam entry fees apply per subject, and centres may charge for administration. Ask for a clear list before you commit.
