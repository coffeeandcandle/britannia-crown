Many parents find modern primary maths unfamiliar. The methods are different, but the aim is deeper understanding.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Schools teach **why** methods work, not just how.
- Common tools include **number lines, bar models and number bonds**.
- Ask your child to **show you** their method.

</Callout>

## Number bonds

Pairs of numbers that make a total, for example 3 and 7 make 10. Knowing bonds to 10 and 20 makes mental maths quicker.

## Number lines

A line that shows numbers in order, used for counting on and back, adding, subtracting and showing fractions. It helps children see what is happening.

## Place value

Understanding that the digits in a number have different values, for example in 345, the 4 means forty. It underpins all arithmetic.

## Bar models

A bar model uses rectangles to show amounts and relationships in word problems. It lets children see the structure of a problem before choosing an operation.

## Partitioning and chunking

Splitting numbers into parts makes calculations easier: 34 + 25 becomes 30 + 20 and 4 + 5. For division, chunking takes away multiples in steps.

## Written methods

Children eventually learn standard written methods such as column addition and subtraction, short and long multiplication and short and long division. The earlier models help them understand what is going on.

## How to help

- Ask your child to explain their method.
- Use the same words the school uses.
- Practise in short sessions.
- If stuck, ask the teacher for examples of methods being taught.
