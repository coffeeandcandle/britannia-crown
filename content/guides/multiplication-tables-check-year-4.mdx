Year 4 children in England take an on-screen check of their times tables. It is short, but fluency matters for later maths.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- It is **on a screen**, with 25 questions in a short time.
- It covers tables **up to 12 times 12**.
- The aim is **fluency**, not a high score.

</Callout>

## What happens

Children answer 25 multiplication questions, each with a few seconds to respond. Before the real check there is a practice session. The check is taken in June in school.

## What it measures

It measures whether children can recall facts quickly, which frees them to focus on harder ideas later in maths.

## Results

There is no pass mark, and the data is used by schools and the government to understand how well tables are being learned. Ask your child's teacher how your child got on.

## Helping at home

- Practise a little each day, in short bursts of five minutes.
- Use games, songs and online practice tools.
- Focus on one table at a time, then mix.
- Spot patterns, such as doubling and the nine times table tricks.
- Praise effort and improvement.

See [how to learn times tables](/guides/how-to-learn-times-tables).

## If your child finds it hard

Do not worry, and do not make it stressful. Ask the teacher for support. A tutor can help with fluency through short, gentle sessions.

## Beyond Year 4
