Many parents who home educate also work. It takes planning and honesty about what is realistic.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **Flexible working and shared roles** make it possible.
- Focused learning needs fewer hours than a school day, but **supervision** is still important.
- Use **tutors, groups and independent learning** to share the load.

</Callout>

## What families do

- **Part-time or flexible work**, such as working from home or around the other parent.
- **Shared teaching** between parents, grandparents or friends.
- **Co-ops** where families teach each other's children.
- **Tutors and classes** for key subjects.

## Planning the week

Block times for teaching, independent study and outside activities. Many families teach in the morning and use afternoons for activities and projects. Younger children need more adult time, while older children can work more independently.

## Independent learning

Older children can follow structured courses, online lessons or workbooks, with check-ins from you. Build routines gradually and review progress.

## Supervision and safety

Children need to be safe and supervised, especially when younger. Think about who is with them when you are working. See [home education and the law](/guides/home-education-legal-requirements-england).

## Money

Reducing work affects income. Plan for the change. See [how much home education costs](/guides/home-education-costs).

## Be realistic

Some seasons will be easier than others. Be ready to adapt, and consider a return to school if circumstances change. See [going back to school after home education](/guides/going-back-to-school-after-home-education).
