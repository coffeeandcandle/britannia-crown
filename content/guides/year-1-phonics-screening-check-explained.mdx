In England, children take a short phonics check in Year 1. It helps schools identify children who need extra reading support.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- The check takes place in **June** of Year 1, one to one with a teacher.
- Children read **40 words**, some of them made up.
- Children who need more help get it and **retake in Year 2**.

</Callout>

## What it is

A short, one-to-one check, taking a few minutes, in which a child reads aloud a list of real words and pseudo-words. It checks whether they can use phonics to decode.

## The pseudo-words

Alongside real words are made-up words, usually shown with a picture of an alien. They ensure children are using phonics, not guessing from memory.

## Scoring

The threshold has usually been 32 out of 40, though the government sets it each year and schools tell parents the result. Children below the threshold receive extra help.

## If your child does not meet the standard

It is not a failure. Teachers use the results to plan extra teaching, and children retake the check in Year 2. Many catch up.

## Helping at home

- Read together every day, even for a few minutes.
- Practise sounds and blending, using whatever scheme the school uses.
- Play word games, such as sounding out words on signs.
- Keep it light. Pressure often backfires.

## Learning to read

Phonics is one part of reading. Vocabulary, stories and talk also matter. See [phonics and learning to read](/guides/phonics-and-learning-to-read-in-england).

## If you are concerned

Speak to the teacher. If progress remains slow, ask about additional support. See [how to help your child read at home](/guides/how-to-help-your-child-read-at-home).
