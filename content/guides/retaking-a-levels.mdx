Not every student gets the A-Level results they hoped for. Retaking can be a good option, but it is worth looking at all the routes before deciding.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Compare **retaking, Clearing and other routes** before deciding.
- Resits are usually in **the summer series**. Plan early.
- **Ask universities** how they treat resit grades.

</Callout>

## Options on results day

Before deciding to retake, consider:

- **Clearing:** places on courses at universities with spaces.
- **A reconsideration:** a review of marking, if the mark was close.
- **A different course or university** where your grades are enough.
- **A gap year** or an apprenticeship.

See [results day: what to do](/guides/results-day-gcse-a-level-what-to-do).

## Retaking in practice

Students can retake A-Levels at their school or college if it offers resits, or at a different provider, or as a private candidate. In the latter case you need to find an exam centre and pay entry fees. See [entering exams as a private candidate](/guides/private-candidate-gcse-how-to-enter) for a similar process.

## Rules and deadlines

Each exam board sets its own entry deadlines, and these usually fall months before the exams. Check with the board and centre well in advance. Any course content changes between years can matter too.

## How universities see resits

Universities have different policies. Some accept resit grades, some ask for higher grades from resit applicants, and some only consider first attempts for certain courses. Contact admissions tutors directly.

## Making a retake work

- Find out **why** the grade was not reached: gaps, exam technique or stress.
- Make a realistic **revision plan** and stick to it.
- Consider a tutor for subjects where marks were lost.

## Costs

Resits may involve exam fees, tuition fees if at a college and sometimes private tutoring. Ask providers for a clear breakdown before enrolling.
