Grammar schools are state-funded secondary schools that select pupils by academic ability, and the entrance test is commonly called the **11-plus**. They exist only in some parts of England, and the process can feel confusing. This guide explains what the 11-plus is, how it works and how to decide whether to prepare.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Grammar schools are **selective state schools**, available only in some areas of England.
- Children take an **entrance test**, usually in Year 6, and a pass does not always guarantee a place.
- **You still apply through your local authority**, and some schools have a separate registration for the test.

</Callout>

## What is a grammar school?

A grammar school is a state secondary school that admits children according to a test of academic ability. They are free to attend. A relatively small number of areas have them, and some areas have **partially selective** schools, which admit a proportion of pupils by ability.

## How the 11-plus works

Tests differ between areas and between schools, so always check your own. Common features:

- The test is usually taken in the **autumn of Year 6**, though timings differ.
- You often must **register** for the test earlier, sometimes in the summer before.
- Papers commonly include **English, maths, verbal reasoning and non-verbal reasoning,** though the mix varies.
- Results are generally released before the national secondary application deadline, so that you can decide whether to list a grammar school.

## Passing the test is not the end

A pass shows that your child has reached the school's standard. If the school is oversubscribed, it ranks children who qualify, using its criteria or the score. Entry can depend on **score ranking, distance or catchment, or other priorities.** A pass does not always guarantee an offer.

You also need to complete your normal secondary application through your **home local authority**, listing the grammar school among your preferences. Some schools also ask for a supplementary form.

## Is the 11-plus right for your child?

Selective schools suit some children very well, but not every child. Consider:

- **Your child's strengths and temperament.** Do they enjoy challenge and cope well with pace?
- **The alternatives.** Are the non-selective schools in your area strong? A great local school can be better for your child than a pressured selective one.
- **Wellbeing.** Prepare in a way that keeps the process calm. The test is one measure, not a judgment of your child.

## Preparing for the 11-plus

Preparation is optional and views differ on how much is sensible. If you decide to prepare:

1. **Find out exactly what the test covers.** Check the school or consortium website and whether the paper is of a certain type.
2. **Start with the basics:** reading widely, fluent number skills and clear writing.
3. **Introduce the format gradually.** Practice papers help children feel at ease with timings and question styles, particularly reasoning questions that look unfamiliar.
4. **Keep it proportionate.** A little regular practice over time is better than frantic cramming.
5. **Look after confidence and sleep.**
