T Levels are technical qualifications for students aged 16 to 19 in England. They sit alongside A-Levels and apprenticeships as an option after GCSEs.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- T Levels are **two-year technical courses** with classroom study and a work placement.
- They cover areas such as health, digital, construction and business.
- They can lead to **work, an apprenticeship or university**.

</Callout>

## What a T Level involves

T Levels are designed with employers. Students study technical content in a school or college and spend a substantial period with an employer on an industry placement. The placement is a required part of the qualification.

## Subject areas

T Levels cover technical routes such as digital, health and science, construction, engineering, education and early years, business and finance and more. Which ones are available depends on the provider, so check with your local colleges.

## Entry requirements

Providers set their own entry requirements. These often include a number of GCSEs, sometimes with particular grades in English and maths. Some providers offer a transition programme for students who need extra preparation.

## What happens next

After a T Level, students can start work, begin a higher-level apprenticeship or go to university. T Levels carry UCAS points, but some courses need specific A-Levels, so check each university.

## How T Levels compare

| | A-Level | T Level | Apprenticeship |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mostly | Classroom, exams | College plus work placement | Work, with training |
| Employed? | No | No | Yes |
| Typical age | 16 to 18 | 16 to 19 | 16 and over |

## Questions to ask

- Which T Levels does the provider offer, and with which employers?
- How is the placement arranged?
- What do students go on to do?
