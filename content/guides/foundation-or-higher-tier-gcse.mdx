In some GCSE subjects, students sit either a Foundation or a Higher tier paper. The tier limits the grades available, so the entry decision matters.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **Foundation** papers allow grades 1 to 5; **Higher** papers allow grades 4 to 9.
- The **school decides** the tier, usually after mock exams.
- A student aiming for **grade 6 or above** needs Higher.

</Callout>

## How tiers work

Maths, most sciences and some other subjects have two tiers. Questions on Foundation papers are generally more accessible and cover the lower grades. Higher papers include harder questions and span the top grades. Some questions overlap, so grades 4 and 5 can be reached from either tier.

## Why the choice matters

Foundation tops out at grade 5. If a student sits Foundation but is capable of more, they cannot reach it. On the other hand, a student on Higher who finds the paper too hard may struggle to reach a grade 4 at all, because the lowest grade on Higher is a narrow 3 at the bottom.

## How schools decide

Teachers usually look at classwork, test results and mock exams. The decision is often made close to the exams, so it can be flexible until entries are submitted. Ask the school when they decide and what evidence they use.

## What parents can do

- Ask your child's teacher which tier they are working towards.
- Find out how close they are to the **boundary** between tiers.
- Consider extra support if your child is on the border. A tutor can help build confidence to allow a Higher entry.
