Families abroad often want British qualifications. Planning ahead makes the process much smoother.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Many students abroad take **IGCSEs** at international centres.
- Private candidates need an **approved exam centre**.
- **Plan at least a year ahead**.

</Callout>

## Qualification choices

IGCSEs and A-Levels are widely available abroad through British international schools and some independent centres. GCSEs are designed for UK schools, though some centres offer them. See [IGCSE vs GCSE](/guides/igcse-vs-gcse).

## Finding a centre

Contact exam boards for lists of centres, or ask local British schools and international centres whether they accept private candidates. Fees and deadlines vary.

## Private candidates

Subjects with coursework or speaking assessments may be harder to enter privately. Choose subjects that centres can supervise. See [entering GCSEs as a private candidate](/guides/private-candidate-gcse-how-to-enter).

## Preparation

Pupils need teaching and revision, just as in school. Online tutoring can fill gaps. See [keeping UK learning on track abroad](/guides/keeping-uk-learning-on-track-abroad).

## Dates and time zones

Exams are usually sat at set times, which may be adjusted for different time zones in some cases. The centre will confirm timings.

## Costs

Expect entry fees, centre charges and possibly travel. Ask for a full list.

## Planning for the UK

If your child plans to return to the UK for sixth form or university, check that their qualifications meet entry requirements.
