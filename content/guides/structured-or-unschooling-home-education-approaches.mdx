Home educators use a wide range of approaches. Understanding them helps you choose or blend what suits your family.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Approaches range from **school-style** to **child-led**.
- Many families are **eclectic**, mixing methods.
- Keep **records**, whichever approach you choose.

</Callout>

## Structured or school at home

Follows a timetable and a curriculum, often with workbooks or a ready-made course. It suits families who want clear structure and progression towards exams.

## Online school and courses

Children study with live or recorded lessons online, sometimes through a provider that offers exam preparation. This can reduce preparation for parents, but costs more.

## Eclectic

Combines resources and methods: for example, structured maths and English with project-based learning in other subjects. This is very common.

## Charlotte Mason and similar

Emphasises short lessons, living books, nature study and narration. It suits families who value reading, discussion and the outdoors.

## Unschooling or child-led learning

Learning follows the child's interests, with little formal structure. It requires parents to provide a rich environment and keep an eye on progress in core skills. Councils may ask how learning takes place, so keep notes and examples.

## Choosing

Ask:

- What are our goals, including exams and the next step?
- How does my child learn best?
- How much time and energy do I have?
- How will I track progress?

See [home education curriculum choices](/guides/home-education-curriculum-choices).

## Whichever you choose

Include core skills such as reading, writing and maths, and record learning in a simple way. See [home education and local authority contact](/guides/home-education-and-local-authority-contact).
