If your child has special educational needs or a disability, understanding how support works helps you ask for the right help.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Most support starts with the **school's SEN support**.
- A small number of children need an **EHC plan**.
- You can **ask for an assessment** yourself.

</Callout>

## SEN support

Schools identify children who need extra help and plan support, often through a cycle known as assess, plan, do, review. The SENCO oversees this. Support might include extra reading help, small groups, adjusted teaching or specialist advice. You should be kept informed.

## Talking to the school

If you are worried, ask to speak to the class teacher and SENCO. Describe what you see at home and what you have noticed. Ask what the school has tried, how it will review progress and how you can help.

## EHC needs assessment

If a child's needs cannot be met through the school's own resources, an EHC needs assessment may be needed. A request can be made to the local authority by the school or by parents. The authority decides whether to assess and whether to issue a plan within set time limits.

## What an EHCP contains

It sets out the child's needs, the outcomes sought and the support to be provided, and names the school. It is reviewed at least annually and can last until age 25 in some cases.

## Your rights

Parents can challenge decisions by mediation and appeal to a tribunal. Independent organisations, such as local information, advice and support services, can help with the process.

## Where tutoring fits

A tutor can complement school support, for example with reading or maths, but should work with the school's plan and cannot replace statutory support. See [dyslexia support at school and home](/guides/dyslexia-support-at-school-and-home).

## Exams

Students with needs may qualify for adjustments in exams. See [exam access arrangements](/guides/exam-access-arrangements-extra-time).
