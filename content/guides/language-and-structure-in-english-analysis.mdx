GCSE English questions often ask about **language** or **structure**. Students sometimes confuse them. The simplest rule is that language is about **words** and structure is about **order and shape**.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **Language**: the words, phrases, images and sentence types a writer chooses.
- **Structure**: how a text is organised, from its opening to its ending.
- **Form**: the type of text and the conventions that come with it.

</Callout>

## Language

Language includes word choice, imagery, tone and sentence length. For example, a writer might choose a harsh verb to create menace, or use short sentences to build tension. Strong answers quote a short phrase and explain its effect.

## Structure

Structure is about how the writer arranges ideas. It includes the order of events, shifts in focus, repetition, changes in pace and how a piece begins and ends. A text might start with a wide view and zoom in to a single detail, and the shift guides the reader's attention.

## Form

Form covers the type of text. A sonnet, a diary entry and a speech each have conventions, and a writer may follow or break them for effect.

## A simple method

1. **Point**: state what the writer does.
2. **Evidence**: quote a short example.
3. **Explain**: say the effect on the reader.
4. **Link**: connect to the whole text or the question.
