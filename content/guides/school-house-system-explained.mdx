In many British schools, pupils belong to a **house**, a smaller community within the school. Houses are named, often with their own colours, and members come from every year group.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Houses mix **pupils across year groups**.
- They are used for **competitions, events and pastoral support**.
- Pupils are **allocated** on joining, often keeping siblings together.

</Callout>

## What houses do

Houses compete in sports days, music and drama events, and academic challenges, with points counted across the year. In many schools they also provide pastoral care, with a head of house who knows pupils well.

## How placement works

Schools usually allocate pupils to a house when they join, often by family so siblings are together, and try to balance numbers. Some boarding schools use houses for accommodation as well.

## Why schools use them

Houses give pupils a sense of belonging, especially in large schools, and an older pupil may mentor younger ones. They can also help new pupils settle in.
