11-plus results are usually given as **standardised scores** rather than raw marks. The score shows how a child performed compared with other children of the same age.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- A standardised score adjusts for **age and test difficulty**, so children are compared fairly.
- An average child scores **around 100**.
- **Pass marks differ** by area and school, and passing does not guarantee a place.

</Callout>

## What "standardised" means

Raw marks are converted so that the average is set at a fixed score, commonly 100, and so that younger children are not disadvantaged compared with older ones. This is why a raw mark alone doesn't tell you much.

## Pass marks and rankings

Some areas set a pass mark, such as a minimum standardised score across the papers. Others rank all candidates and offer places to the highest scoring until the school is full. Your target school's website explains which applies.

## Why a good score may not be enough

In oversubscribed schools, qualifying is only the first step. Places are then offered by the admissions criteria, which may include distance, siblings or the highest scores. See [how school admissions criteria work](/guides/how-school-admissions-criteria-work).
