If you have decided to home educate a child who is on a school roll, you need to formally deregister them. The process is usually straightforward.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Write to the **head teacher** to say you are withdrawing your child.
- The school **removes your child from the roll** and informs the council.
- Special school pupils and some other cases need **extra steps**.

</Callout>

## Before you decide

Think carefully and, if possible, discuss concerns with the school. Look at what home education involves, and consider your child's views. See [home education and the law](/guides/home-education-legal-requirements-england).

## How to deregister

1. **Write a letter or email** to the head teacher saying that you are withdrawing your child from school to home educate, with the date.
2. **Keep a copy** and ask for acknowledgement.
3. The school must **remove the child from the roll** and tell the local authority.

You do not need to give detailed reasons, and the school should not ask you to prove your plans.

## Exceptions

If your child is at a special school under local authority arrangements, you usually need the council's consent. Children with an EHC plan should have their plan reviewed, so contact the local authority.

## Exams and records

Ask the school for your child's records, work and results. If your child is in Year 10 or 11, consider exam entry carefully. See [GCSEs for home educated children](/guides/qualifications-for-home-educated-children).

## Timing

You can deregister at any time. Some families choose a natural break, though that is not required. Avoid leaving it late if a child is unhappy.

## After deregistering

The local authority may contact you. See [home education and local authority contact](/guides/home-education-and-local-authority-contact). If you later want a school place, see [going back to school after home education](/guides/going-back-to-school-after-home-education).
