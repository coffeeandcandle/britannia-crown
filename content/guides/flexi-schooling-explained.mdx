Flexi-schooling means a child is registered at a school but attends part of the week, and is educated at home for the rest. It can suit some families, but it is not a right, and schools differ in whether they agree.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Flexi-schooling is **an arrangement agreed with the school**, not a legal entitlement.
- **The head teacher decides**, and many schools say no.
- The child stays on the school roll, so it is different from [home education](/guides/home-education-england).

</Callout>

## How it works

Your child is enrolled at a school and attends on agreed days or sessions. For the rest of the time you take responsibility for their education. The pattern is agreed with the school and should be written down.

## Is there a right to it?

No. Parents cannot require a school to agree. The head teacher decides, and may refuse. Government guidance treats it as a matter for the school's discretion, so check the current guidance on gov.uk.

## Why families consider it

- A child who finds a full week overwhelming.
- Families who want to home educate part of the time, for example for a specialist interest.
- A gradual return to school, which our guide to [going back to school after home education](/guides/going-back-to-school-after-home-education) covers.

## Questions to ask the school

- What is its policy, and has it agreed flexi-schooling before?
- Which days or sessions would be at school, and how would they be recorded?
- How will the school share work, assessment and progress?
- How long will the arrangement last, and when will it be reviewed?

## Points to weigh

Flexi-schooling needs steady commitment at home and a good relationship with the school. Time at home is your responsibility to plan. If a school refuses, you can still choose between full attendance and [deregistering your child](/guides/deregistering-your-child-from-school).
