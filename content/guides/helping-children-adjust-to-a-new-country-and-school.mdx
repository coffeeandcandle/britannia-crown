A move to a new country can be exciting and unsettling, especially for children. A little preparation and patience help.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Talk openly about **feelings and expectations**.
- Keep **routines and familiar things**.
- Stay in touch with the **school** and watch for signs of distress.

</Callout>

## Before the move

Involve children in the planning. Look at photos of the new home and school, and talk about what will change and what will stay the same. Let them say goodbye to friends, with plans to stay in touch.

## After arrival

- Establish **routines** quickly: meals, bedtimes and activities.
- Keep familiar objects, music and traditions.
- Explore the area together.
- Visit the school and meet the teacher before starting if possible.

## Friendships

Help children find clubs and activities. Arrange playdates with classmates. Be patient: friendships take time.

## School change

Share information with the new school, including strengths, interests and any needs. Ask how they help new pupils settle. Check in with teachers after the first weeks.

## Learning gaps

Different curricula can cause gaps and repetition. A tutor can help bridge gaps and keep learning on track. See [keeping UK learning on track abroad](/guides/keeping-uk-learning-on-track-abroad).

## Looking after yourself

Parents also feel the strain of a move. Looking after your own wellbeing helps children.

## When to get help

If distress continues, speak to the school and your doctor. Counselling or other support may help.
