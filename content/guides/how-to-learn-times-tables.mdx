Knowing times tables makes almost everything in maths easier. Short, regular practice usually works better than occasional long sessions.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Practise **five minutes a day**, rather than long sessions.
- Learn tables in a **sensible order** and use patterns.
- **Mix** tables once each feels secure.

</Callout>

## A sensible order

1. 2, 5 and 10.
2. 3 and 4.
3. 6, 7, 8 and 9.
4. 11 and 12.

The tables share patterns, so later ones are easier if earlier ones are secure.

## Understanding first

Show what multiplication means using objects, arrays and number lines, so the facts have meaning. Then move to memorising.

## Patterns and tricks

- Doubling for 2 and 4.
- Counting in fives for 5, and halving the tens for 5s.
- For 9s, the digits of the answer add up to 9 up to 9 times 10.
- Commutativity: 3 times 7 is the same as 7 times 3, halving the facts to learn.

## Practice ideas

- **Chanting and songs** for those who like rhythm.
- **Flashcards** that you can mix.
- **Games:** dice, cards and board games with multiplication.
- **Short online quizzes**, with a time limit as skill grows.
- **Real life:** shopping, cooking and sharing.

## Making it stick

Return to old tables regularly, mix tables, and ask both ways: "What is 6 times 7?" and "How many sevens in 42?" Quick recall comes from repeated, spaced practice.

## When it feels hard

Slow down, go back to a table that is secure and build up again. Praise progress. See [Year 4 multiplication tables check](/guides/multiplication-tables-check-year-4).
