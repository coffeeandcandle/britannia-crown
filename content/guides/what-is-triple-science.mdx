**Triple science**, also called separate sciences, means studying **biology, chemistry and physics as three distinct GCSEs**. Each gets its own grade.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Triple science gives **three GCSEs**, one for each science.
- Combined science gives **two GCSEs** covering all three sciences.
- Schools decide who can take it and how much teaching time it needs.

</Callout>

## How it differs from combined science

Combined science covers the three sciences in one qualification worth two GCSEs, graded with a double grade such as 5-5. Triple science goes into more depth and usually needs extra lesson time. See the full comparison in [combined or triple science](/guides/gcse-science-combined-or-separate).

## Who it suits

Triple science suits students who enjoy science and are comfortable with the maths involved. It is often offered as an option or to students on a particular pathway, and each school sets its own policy.

## A-Level implications

Combined science can lead to A-Level sciences, but some sixth forms prefer or require separate sciences for A-Level Chemistry and Physics. Check entry requirements early. See [A-Level subject choices](/guides/a-level-subject-choices-and-university).
