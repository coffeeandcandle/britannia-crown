A first tutoring lesson can feel nerve-wracking for children and parents alike. Knowing what usually happens makes it easier to prepare, and to judge afterwards whether the tutor is right.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- The first lesson is mostly about **getting to know your child** and finding gaps.
- Expect questions about school, goals and how your child likes to learn.
- Afterwards, you should have a **clear idea of the plan** and next steps.

</Callout>

## Before the lesson

Share useful background in advance: your child's year group, exam board, recent grades or reports, and what they find hard. Tell your child what to expect in simple terms: this is a chance to ask questions and get help, not a test.

For online lessons, check the device, internet and a quiet space. See [how to set up for online tutoring at home](/guides/how-to-set-up-for-online-tutoring-at-home).

## What usually happens

**A conversation.** A good tutor starts by chatting to your child about school, interests and what they find easy or difficult.

**A light check of current level.** The tutor may ask your child to attempt a few questions or talk through some recent work. This is to find gaps, not to catch them out.

**Some real learning.** Even in a first session, most children should leave having understood or practised something specific.

**A plan.** By the end, the tutor should explain what they noticed and what they suggest working on first.

## What a good first lesson feels like

- Your child **speaks, tries and asks questions**, rather than just listening.
- The tutor explains things in more than one way if needed.
- The tone is **encouraging but honest**.
- You are told clearly what happens next.

## Questions to ask afterwards

- What did you notice about my child's strengths and gaps?
- What will you work on over the next few weeks?
- How will you tell me about progress?
- Is there anything my child should practise between lessons?

## When to try someone else

Rapport matters. If your child dreads sessions or the tutor is unclear about plans, raise it early. A good tutor or agency will take this well. Our free consultation includes help with matching, so you are not left guessing.
