Finding out that a child has fallen behind is worrying, but it is more common than parents expect. With the right support, most children can close gaps and regain confidence. This guide explains how.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **Find out where** the gap starts, which is often earlier than the current topic.
- **Talk to the school first** and rule out any underlying need.
- Rebuild **confidence first**, then speed.

</Callout>

## Why children fall behind

Common reasons include missed school through illness or moving, a stretch of poor teaching or a change of school, a confidence dip, or a difficulty that has not been spotted. Sometimes one missed idea in Year 3 causes trouble years later.

## Step 1: Talk to the school

Ask the class or subject teacher where the gaps are and how they are supporting your child. If you are concerned about a possible learning difficulty, ask to speak to the SENCO. See also our guide to [signs your child needs a tutor](/guides/signs-your-child-needs-a-tutor).

## Step 2: Find the root of the gap

Going back to foundations can feel like a step backwards, but it is often the quickest route forward. A good tutor will check earlier skills, such as times tables, reading fluency or sentence structure, before pushing on.

## Step 3: Small, regular steps

Short, regular sessions beat occasional long ones. Aim for visible wins early on, so your child sees that they can do it. Celebrate effort, not just results.

## Step 4: Keep school in the loop

Share what the tutor is working on with the teacher, and ask them to share what the class is covering. This stops the two getting out of step.

## What not to do

- Avoid comparing your child with siblings or friends.
- Do not turn every evening into extra work. Rest matters.
- Do not wait until a big exam is close if you can start sooner.

## Confidence matters

A child who believes they are "bad at" a subject often stops trying. A tutor who is patient, encouraging and honest can change that. Look for someone who listens as well as explains.
