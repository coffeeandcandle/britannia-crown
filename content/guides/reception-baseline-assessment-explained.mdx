In England, children take a short assessment early in Reception. It is not a test to worry about, but it is useful to understand.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- It is **short and practical**, done one to one with a teacher.
- It covers **early maths and communication, language and literacy**.
- It is used to measure **school progress**, not to judge your child.

</Callout>

## What it is

The Reception baseline assessment is a statutory assessment in England, taken within the first weeks of a child starting Reception. The tasks are simple activities, such as counting objects or talking about pictures, and take around twenty minutes.

## What it covers

It looks at early mathematics, and communication, language and literacy. It does not test everything a child can do.

## Why it exists

The results are used to create a starting point against which the school's progress with the pupils can be measured by the end of Key Stage 2. It is a measure of the school, not of the child.

## Do children need preparing?

No. It is meant to show where children are at the start. Cramming or practising can make it harder to interpret. The best preparation is ordinary conversation, stories and play.

## Results

Schools are not required to report individual scores to parents, and no pass or fail applies. Teachers use their daily observations to plan.

## If you have concerns

Speak to the class teacher about your child's development and how they are settling. See [EYFS explained](/guides/eyfs-explained).
