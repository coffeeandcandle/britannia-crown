Applying to Oxford or Cambridge is demanding, and the process starts earlier than for most universities. Parents play a supporting role, and early preparation helps.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- The deadline is **earlier** than most UCAS deadlines.
- Many courses need an **admissions test** and an **interview**.
- Support your child by **encouraging wider reading and keeping dates**.

</Callout>

## The process

Applications go through UCAS, with an early deadline in mid-October. Students choose a course and, in many cases, a college. Many courses ask for an admissions test, and shortlisted students are invited to interview.

## Timeline

- **Year 12:** explore courses, read widely, take part in subject enrichment.
- **Summer before Year 13:** start the personal statement and research admissions tests.
- **Autumn of Year 13:** submit by the early deadline, take tests and prepare for interviews.

## Admissions tests

Tests differ by course and university. They check skills such as problem solving, critical thinking and subject knowledge. Check each course page for the test, registration deadlines and past papers.

## Interviews

Interviews are academic conversations. Tutors want to see how students think about unfamiliar problems, not whether they can recite facts. Practising talking through ideas helps.

## How parents can help

- Encourage **wider reading and curiosity** in the subject.
- Help with **practical matters**: deadlines, test registration, travel.
- Discuss ideas with your child, as a practice for interview.
- Keep perspective: many strong students are not offered places, and many excellent courses exist elsewhere.

## Realistic planning

Include other choices on the UCAS form in case Oxbridge does not work out. Students can apply to five courses in total.
