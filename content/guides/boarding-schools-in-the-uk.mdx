Boarding can suit some children very well, but it is a major decision. This guide explains the basics.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Boarding can be **full, weekly or flexi**.
- It is usually offered by **independent schools**, with some state boarding schools.
- Visit, and ask your child how they feel.

</Callout>

## Types of boarding

**Full boarding.** Children live at school during term, often with some exeat weekends at home.

**Weekly boarding.** Children board Monday to Friday and go home at weekends.

**Flexi boarding.** Children stay a night or more when needed.

## Age and entry

Many pupils board from 11 or 13. Some prep schools take younger boarders. Entry follows the school's own process, which may include assessments and interviews. See [independent school entry](/guides/independent-school-entry-assessments-and-bursaries).

## Who it suits

Boarding can suit children who are independent, enjoy activities and community and thrive on routine. It may not suit a child who is very anxious about being away from home. Listen to your child, and do not push if they are unsure.

## Things to ask

- Who looks after the children out of lessons?
- What is the **pastoral care** like, and who can children talk to?
- How often can children go home, and how do they keep in touch?
- What are the **total costs**?
- How are homesickness and friendship issues handled?

## State boarding schools

A few state schools offer boarding, charging for boarding but not for tuition. Places are limited.

## International families

Many schools welcome pupils from overseas and offer guardianship arrangements. Check immigration rules and school requirements well in advance. See [returning to the UK with children](/guides/returning-to-the-uk-with-children-school-places).

## Visit and review

Visit during term time, talk to boarders and look at the boarding houses. Review regularly after your child starts.
