In England, most children start Reception in the **September after their fourth birthday**. This guide explains how the age rules work and what flexibility exists.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Children born between **1 September and 31 August** start Reception together, the September after they turn four.
- School is **compulsory from the term after a child's fifth birthday**, so Reception itself is optional.
- Summer-born children may be able to **start a year later**, but must apply.

</Callout>

## How the school year works

The school year runs from 1 September to 31 August. A child who turns four between 1 September and 31 August starts Reception that September. A child born on 1 September will be among the oldest in the class and one born on 31 August the youngest.

## When is school compulsory?

A child must be in full-time education from the term after their fifth birthday. That means a child born in the spring or summer may reach compulsory age during Reception, and a child born in the autumn reaches it by the start of Year 1 at the latest. Most families choose Reception regardless.

## Summer-born children

Children born between 1 April and 31 August are the youngest in their year. Parents can ask for a place in Reception a year later, so the child starts at five. See [summer-born children and starting school](/guides/summer-born-children-and-starting-school) for how requests work.

## Applying for a place

Applications for a September start are made through your local authority, and the national closing date is usually 15 January. See our guide to [school admissions in England](/guides/school-admissions-england).
