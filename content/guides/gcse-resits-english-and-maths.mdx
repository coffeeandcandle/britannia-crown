If your child did not achieve a grade 4 in GCSE English or maths, they have not run out of options. There are clear routes to improve, and in England most young people will be expected to keep studying these subjects after 16. This guide explains how resits work and how to prepare.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- In England, students aged 16 to 18 in post-16 education who have not reached a **grade 4** in GCSE English language or maths are generally expected to **keep studying** the subject.
- Those who got a **grade 3** are normally expected to retake the GCSE. Those with a **grade 2 or below** may study a different qualification such as Functional Skills.
- There are **resit opportunities** in the summer and, for English language and maths, in November.

</Callout>

## Why grade 4 matters

A grade 4 is a "standard pass" and a grade 5 a "strong pass". Many sixth forms, colleges, apprenticeships and employers ask for a 4 in English and maths, and universities often require them. This is why the grade is treated as a milestone. Our guide to [GCSE grades](/guides/gcse-grades-explained) explains the scale.

## Who has to resit

The details depend on the student's age, circumstances and the education provider, so check with the sixth form or college. In general in England:

- students who achieved a **grade 3** are expected to study towards a GCSE resit;
- students with a **grade 2 or lower** can be enrolled on a different qualification (such as Functional Skills) as a stepping stone, or on a GCSE;
- the requirement does not apply in the same way to every student, and there are exceptions.

## When can they resit?

GCSE English language and maths can be resat in the **November** series as well as the main **summer** series (May to June). Other subjects are normally resat in the summer only. Entry deadlines are set by the exam centre, and they fall well before the exams, so speak to the centre early.

## How to improve

**Find out exactly where marks were lost.** Ask the school for the paper breakdown if you can, or look at the exam board's examiner reports. Often a few topics or techniques account for most of the gap.

**Fix the gaps in understanding first,** then practise exam technique. In maths that means revisiting the basics and doing past papers. In English language it means practising how to read the question, plan, structure and write to the mark scheme.

**Use past papers and mark schemes.** They show what examiners reward.

**Plan the time.** Resits sit alongside other courses, so a realistic weekly routine matters.

## A resit timeline

The exact dates depend on your exam centre and board, but a typical year looks like this:

- **Results day (August):** find out the grade, and talk to the sixth form or college about the plan.
- **September to October:** start re-studying, and confirm entry for the **November** series if your child is aiming for it. Ask about the entry deadline straight away.
- **November:** English language and maths can be resat. Results usually arrive in the new year.
- **January to April:** continue to work towards the **summer** series if needed, using the first results to see where marks remain.
- **May to June:** summer exams. Results follow in August.

Because entry deadlines for November come soon after results day, it is worth ringing the centre in August.

## Which route suits which student?

| Situation | What commonly happens | Questions to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Grade 3 in English or maths | Expected to study towards a GCSE resit | Which series are we aiming for, and what support is there? |
| Grade 2 or below | May study a stepping-stone qualification such as Functional Skills, or a GCSE | Which route will lead to a grade 4 soonest? |
| Grade 4 or above in both | No resit needed for this requirement | Is the grade enough for the next course? |
| Close to a pass | A focused plan can make a large difference | Which topics or skills account for most of the gap? |

Rules and options vary, so confirm with the college or sixth form.

## A ten-week catch-up plan

This sample plan assumes about 10 weeks before an exam series. Adapt it to the subject.

1. **Week 1:** analyse the previous paper. List the topics and skills where marks were lost.
2. **Weeks 2 to 4:** rebuild understanding of the biggest gaps. In maths that might be fractions, percentages, algebra or ratio. In English language it might be summarising, analysing language or structuring writing.
3. **Weeks 5 to 7:** practise mixed questions under mild time pressure, marking against the mark scheme.
4. **Weeks 8 to 9:** full past papers in exam conditions, then review mistakes.
5. **Week 10:** light revision, rest, and logistics such as equipment, timings and the route to the exam.

Short, regular sessions beat last-minute cramming. A tutor can keep the plan on track and focus on the highest-value skills.

## What if it does not go to plan?

A resit that falls short is disappointing, but there are usually further opportunities in later series. Keep a record of what improved, and adjust the plan. Talk to the college about the next step, and keep your child's confidence intact. See [supporting a child through exam stress](/guides/how-to-support-a-child-through-exam-stress).
