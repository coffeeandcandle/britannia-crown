Parents often weigh up one-to-one tutoring against group classes. Each has strengths, and the best choice depends on your child's needs, personality and budget.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **One-to-one** gives tailored pace and attention. It costs more per child.
- **Small groups** can be cheaper and sociable, but pace is shared.
- Think about **gaps, confidence and goals** before deciding.

</Callout>

## One-to-one tutoring

**Strengths:**

- Lessons fit your child's exact level and pace.
- Children who are shy are more likely to ask questions.
- The tutor can adapt quickly if something is not working.
- Scheduling is flexible.

**Limitations:**

- It usually costs more per child.
- Some children miss the energy and competition of a group.

## Group tutoring

**Strengths:**

- Cost is shared, so the price per child is lower.
- Peers can motivate each other, and hearing other children's questions helps.
- It can feel less intense for some children.

**Limitations:**

- Pace is set for the group, so gaps can go unaddressed.
- Quieter children may say little.
- Timings are fixed.

## Which suits which child?

**One-to-one often suits** children with significant gaps, children who lack confidence, those preparing for a specific exam or those with particular learning needs.

**Groups often suit** children who are doing well and want revision or practice, those who thrive in company and families on a tighter budget.
