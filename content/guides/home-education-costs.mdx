Home education costs vary enormously. Some families spend very little, while others invest in tutors and courses.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- There is **no government funding** for home education in England.
- Costs depend on **approach, resources, exams and tutoring**.
- Plan for **income changes** as well as spending.

</Callout>

## Typical costs

- **Resources:** books, stationery, workbooks, software and subscriptions.
- **Activities:** clubs, trips, swimming, music, sport and classes.
- **Exams:** entry fees per subject and exam centre charges.
- **Tutors or courses:** for subjects you do not want to teach.
- **Space and equipment:** such as a computer and a desk.

## Saving money

- Use the library, second-hand books and free online resources.
- Share resources with other families.
- Join home education groups for discounted trips and shared classes.
- Choose IGCSEs and exam centres carefully, as fees differ.

## The hidden cost

When a parent reduces work to teach, household income may fall. Think about how you will manage, and whether flexible or part-time work is possible. See [home education and working parents](/guides/home-education-and-working-parents).

## Budgeting

Make a simple annual budget with essentials first. Review it each term, since needs change, especially around exam years.

## Tutors and support

Tutoring costs vary by subject and experience, and a few well-chosen sessions can fill gaps. See [how much a private tutor costs](/guides/how-much-does-a-private-tutor-cost-uk).

## Value for money

Consider what your child needs rather than buying everything. Often, time, conversation and access to books matter most.
