In Year 6, children sit tests in grammar, punctuation and spelling as part of SATs. The terms can be unfamiliar, but the skills help all writing.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- There are **two papers**: grammar and punctuation, and spelling.
- Children learn **grammar terms** such as clauses, adverbials and conjunctions.
- Reading and writing practice helps most.

</Callout>

## The papers

One paper has short questions on grammar and punctuation. The other is a spelling test of twenty words read aloud. They are taken in May alongside other Key Stage 2 tests.

## Common terms

**Noun, verb, adjective, adverb.** The word classes.

**Conjunction.** A word that joins clauses, such as 'and', 'but' or 'because'.

**Clause.** A group of words with a subject and verb.

**Fronted adverbial.** A word or phrase at the start of a sentence, such as 'Later that day,'.

**Apostrophe.** For contraction (do not becomes don't) and possession (the dog's bone).

## Helping with grammar

- Ask children to explain terms in their own words.
- Spot examples when reading.
- Play word games: change a word class, extend a sentence.
- Use short practice questions regularly.

## Helping with spelling

- Use the school's spelling list.
- Learn rules and patterns, such as adding 'ing' or 'ed'.
- Say, cover, write and check.
- Use mnemonics for tricky words.

## Writing

Writing practice helps grammar and punctuation settle. Encourage short pieces, such as diary entries, letters or stories, and talk about how to improve them.

## Keeping it balanced

The test is one measure. Keep practice short and positive. See [KS2 SATs explained](/guides/ks2-sats-explained).
