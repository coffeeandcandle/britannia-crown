Every GCSE is set and marked by an exam board, also called an awarding body. Knowing which board your child's school uses helps with revision, tutoring and past papers.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- The main boards in England are **AQA, Pearson Edexcel, OCR and WJEC Eduqas**.
- A school may use **different boards for different subjects**.
- Always check the **exact specification** before choosing revision materials.

</Callout>

## The main boards

**AQA.** One of the largest, offering a wide range of subjects.

**Pearson Edexcel.** Also offers a broad range, and runs International GCSEs.

**OCR.** Offers many subjects, with some distinctive options such as Gateway and Twenty First Century Science.

**WJEC Eduqas.** The England arm of the Welsh board WJEC, offering a number of subjects.

## What differs between boards

The national subject content is set by the Department for Education, so all boards cover similar material. They differ in:

- how papers are structured and timed;
- the style of questions;
- set texts in English Literature and some other subjects;
- optional topics in subjects such as history, geography and religious studies.

## How to find your child's board

Ask the school or check the subject booklet. The specification code, for example "8300" for AQA maths, helps you find the exact materials. You can also look at exam entries or past papers your child has sat.

## Why it matters

- **Past papers and mark schemes** differ by board.
- **Set texts and topics** need to match.
- A tutor who knows the board can teach the right content and style.

## Private candidates and IGCSEs

Private candidates, and some independent schools, may use International GCSEs, which are separate qualifications. See [IGCSE vs GCSE](/guides/igcse-vs-gcse) and [entering GCSEs as a private candidate](/guides/private-candidate-gcse-how-to-enter).
