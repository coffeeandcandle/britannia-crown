Grade boundaries confuse many parents. They are the number of marks needed to reach each GCSE grade, and they change from year to year. Understanding them helps you read results sensibly, and stops needless worry before exams.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- A boundary is the **minimum mark** needed for a grade on a particular paper.
- Boundaries are **set after marking**, so they cannot be known in advance.
- They **vary by subject, board and year**, so be wary of using last year's figures as a target.

</Callout>

## How marks become grades

Students sit exam papers that add up to a total mark. After marking, the exam board decides how many marks are needed for each grade from 9 to 1. These cut-off scores are the grade boundaries.

## Why boundaries move

Some papers are slightly harder or easier than others. Setting boundaries afterwards helps keep a grade comparable from year to year. Examiners look at the paper, the quality of answers and statistical evidence about how the cohort performed.

## Different tiers, different boundaries

In subjects with Foundation and Higher tiers, such as maths and science, each tier has its own boundaries. Higher tier grades go up to 9, and a student who just misses a grade 4 on the Higher paper may still get a grade 3, but the boundaries are different from the Foundation paper.

## What parents can use boundaries for

Past boundaries give a rough idea of the range, but they are not a target. A student who scores at last year's boundary might fall just under or over this year. It is better to aim comfortably above and focus on exam technique.

## Where to find them

Exam boards publish boundaries for each series on their websites. Search for the specification your child's school uses, for example AQA, Pearson Edexcel or OCR. See [GCSE exam boards](/guides/gcse-exam-boards-aqa-edexcel-ocr).

## What to do with this

- Use past papers and mark schemes to see how marks are earned.
- Do not fixate on a single percentage.
- If a result is close to a boundary, ask the school about a [review of marking](/guides/exam-reviews-of-marking-and-appeals).
