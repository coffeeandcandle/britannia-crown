Faith schools can be state-funded schools with a religious character. Their admission rules often differ from other schools, so read them carefully.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Many faith schools **prioritise families who practise** the faith when oversubscribed.
- You often need a **supplementary form** as well as your council application.
- Deadlines can be **earlier** than the standard one.

</Callout>

## What a faith school is

Some state schools have a religious character, for example Church of England, Roman Catholic, Jewish, Muslim or Sikh schools. They follow the national admissions rules, but oversubscribed schools may prioritise applicants by faith, as set out in their admission policy.

## How priority works

A typical policy ranks groups in order, for example children in care, then those from practising families, then others. It may also include siblings and distance. Read the exact wording.

## Evidence of faith

Schools may ask for evidence such as a letter from a religious leader or a record of attendance at services over a period. The policy explains what counts. Start gathering evidence early.

## Supplementary forms and deadlines

Schools often ask for a supplementary form, sometimes with an earlier deadline than the council's. Missing it may affect your ranking, so check each school.

## What if you are not of the faith?

Some schools admit children without a faith background if places remain, and some do not prioritise by faith at all. Look at the policy, and previous years' admissions if available.

## Making the application

Apply through your local authority and complete any additional forms. List your preferences, including schools that are not faith based, in case you are not offered your first choice.

## Appeals

You have the same right to appeal. See [school admission appeals in England](/guides/school-admission-appeals-england).
