Combined science is worth **two GCSEs**, so results are given as a **double grade**, such as 5-5 or 6-5. The grade boundaries use a 17-point scale.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- A double grade shows the result for **two GCSEs** from one course.
- Grades range from **9-9 down to 1-1**, with grades like 5-4 in between.
- The two numbers are either the **same or one grade apart**.

</Callout>

## How the two grades are worked out

The total marks across all the science papers are converted to one of 17 combined outcomes, and each outcome has a pair of grades, such as 6-6, 6-5 and 5-5. Because the pairs are close, the two numbers are never more than one grade apart.

## What counts as a pass

A grade 4-4 is a standard pass and 5-5 a strong pass, in the same way as single GCSEs. See [what counts as a pass](/guides/what-is-a-pass-in-gcse).

## Sixth form and science A-Levels

Sixth forms set their own requirements, and many accept good combined science grades for A-Level sciences, though some prefer separate sciences. Check each entry requirement. See [combined or triple science](/guides/gcse-science-combined-or-separate) and [what triple science is](/guides/what-is-triple-science).
