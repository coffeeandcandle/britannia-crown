Maths anxiety is a real worry or tension around maths, and it can stop children doing what they are capable of. It is common and **can be improved**.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- It shows as **avoidance, negative self-talk or panic** around maths.
- It is **not a sign of low ability**.
- Calm, regular practice and positive talk help.

</Callout>

## Signs to look for

- Saying "I'm rubbish at maths".
- Avoiding homework or becoming upset about it.
- Rushing, guessing or freezing in tests.
- Physical signs such as stomach aches on maths days.

## What helps at home

- **Watch your own language.** Avoid saying you were bad at maths.
- **Praise effort and thinking**, not only right answers.
- **Short, regular practice**, so maths becomes familiar.
- **Use real situations**, like cooking, shopping and games.
- **Ask how they worked it out**, so mistakes become part of learning.

## Rebuilding foundations

Anxiety often follows gaps. Secure times tables and number fluency can restore confidence. See [how to learn times tables](/guides/how-to-learn-times-tables) and [helping with maths at home](/guides/how-to-help-your-child-with-maths-at-home).

## Extra support

If worry persists, talk to the teacher. A patient tutor can rebuild confidence at your child's pace. See [tutoring for a child who is behind](/guides/tutoring-for-a-child-who-is-behind) and [how to motivate a reluctant learner](/guides/how-to-motivate-a-reluctant-learner).
