GCSE English Language is assessed through **two exam papers**, each worth 50% of the grade. The structure below describes AQA, the most common board. Check your child's board, as others differ.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **Paper 1** is about fiction: reading an extract, then creative writing.
- **Paper 2** is about non-fiction: comparing two sources, then writing to argue or explain.
- Each paper has a **reading section and a writing section**, worth equal marks.

</Callout>

## Paper 1: fiction and creative writing

Students read one fiction extract and answer questions on language, structure and evaluation. They then write a descriptive or narrative piece, often prompted by a picture or statement. Reading and writing each carry 40 marks.

## Paper 2: viewpoints and perspectives

Students read two non-fiction sources, often from different time periods, and answer questions on what they say and how they say it, including a comparison. They then write a piece that expresses a viewpoint, such as a letter, speech or article.

## How marks are earned

Longer questions are marked against levels that reward detailed analysis and precise evidence. Writing marks split between content and organisation, and technical accuracy in spelling, punctuation and grammar.
