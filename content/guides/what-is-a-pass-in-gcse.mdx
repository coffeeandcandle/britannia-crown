At GCSE, a **grade 4 is a standard pass** and a **grade 5 is a strong pass**. These are the benchmarks schools, colleges and employers most often use.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **Grade 4** is the standard pass, broadly the old low C.
- **Grade 5** is a strong pass, used by some schools and employers as the higher mark.
- Students without a 4 in **English language or maths** are generally expected to keep studying them after 16.

</Callout>

## Why grade 4 matters

Many sixth forms, colleges, apprenticeships and employers ask for a grade 4 in English and maths. It also affects whether a student must resit. See [GCSE resits in English and maths](/guides/gcse-resits-english-and-maths).

## Why grade 5 matters

Grade 5 is often described as a strong pass, and schools may use it in performance measures. Some courses and employers ask for it, so read each entry requirement.

## What about grades 1 to 3?

These are not passes, but they still show achievement and can be built on. Students with a grade 3 in English or maths are generally expected to retake the GCSE, while those with a 2 or below may take another qualification such as Functional Skills.

## Sixth form entry

Sixth forms set their own rules, often asking for grades 5 to 7 in subjects students want to study at A-Level. See [how to choose a sixth form or college](/guides/how-to-choose-a-sixth-form-or-college) and [GCSE grades explained](/guides/gcse-grades-explained) for the full 9 to 1 scale.
