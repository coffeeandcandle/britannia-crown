Online GCSE tutoring works well for many students. The quality of the **tutor** and the student's engagement matter more than the setting.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Online tutoring can be as effective as face to face for most GCSE students.
- It offers a **wider choice of tutors** and flexible scheduling.
- It needs a **quiet space**, a **good connection** and a way to share written work.

</Callout>

## Why it works

Students can work with a specialist regardless of location, lessons fit around the timetable, and shared digital whiteboards make it easy to work through problems together. Lessons can be recorded for revision if agreed.

## What makes it work

- A **tutor trained in online teaching**, who keeps the student engaged.
- A **quiet, distraction-free space**.
- Shared tools such as a whiteboard or tablet.
- Clear goals and regular feedback.

## When face to face may suit better

Some younger children, or students who find screens tiring, prefer in-person sessions. See [online vs face-to-face tutoring](/guides/online-vs-face-to-face-tutoring).
