Key Stage 3 covers Years 7, 8 and 9, when children are aged 11 to 14. It is a key time for building skills before GCSEs.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Pupils study a **broad range of subjects**.
- There are **no national tests**, but schools track progress.
- Strong foundations now make **GCSEs easier**.

</Callout>

## What children study

Typically English, maths, science, history, geography, languages, computing, art, design and technology, music, PE and RE. Schools may offer variations.

## Assessment

Schools run tests and assessments, and report progress through grades, levels or comments. Ask how your child's school reports and how to read the reports.

## Why it matters

Content builds quickly in subjects like maths and languages, so gaps in Year 7 or 8 can cause trouble later. It is also when study habits and attitudes form.

## Common challenges

- Dips in motivation or confidence.
- Homework becoming more demanding.
- Friendship changes.
- Choosing GCSE options.

## How parents can help

- Keep in touch with teachers.
- Encourage reading and curiosity.
- Support routines for homework and sleep.
- Notice early signs of difficulty. See [signs your child needs a tutor](/guides/signs-your-child-needs-a-tutor).

## Looking ahead

GCSE options are often chosen towards the end of Key Stage 3. See [how to choose GCSE options](/guides/how-to-choose-gcse-options).
