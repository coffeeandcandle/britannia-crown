Children born between April and August are the youngest in their year, and some parents wonder whether to start them later. Rules and process matter here.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Summer-born children are born **1 April to 31 August**.
- Parents can **ask** to start Reception a year later.
- Apply in the **normal round** and give reasons.

</Callout>

## Compulsory school age

A child must be in full-time education from the term after their fifth birthday, though most start in Reception in September after turning four. For summer-born children, that means they may be of compulsory school age only after Reception has started.

## Asking for delayed entry

Parents can ask for their child to start Reception a year later. The admission authority must consider each request properly, and may agree or refuse. Some local authorities have clear processes, so check yours early.

## What to include

Explain why you think delay is right. Evidence such as nursery reports, notes from professionals and details of your child's development helps.

## Applying

You normally apply during the usual admissions round in the autumn, and ask for the delay at the same time. If the request is agreed, you then apply for Year 1 the following year, with a place held. If refused, you can accept a place or apply again.

## Thinking it through

- Consider your child's readiness, social development and likely learning needs.
- Speak to the nursery and the school.
- Think about how the later start affects later stages, including exams.

Teachers are used to working with a range of ages in the same class. See [school readiness: what it really means](/guides/school-readiness-what-it-means).
