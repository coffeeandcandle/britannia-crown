There is no single national rule for A-Level entry. **Each sixth form or college sets its own GCSE requirements**, and they differ by subject.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Requirements are set **by the school or college**, not nationally.
- Many ask for a **grade 4 or 5 in English and maths** overall and a higher grade in the subject itself.
- **Maths and sciences** usually have the highest requirements.

</Callout>

## What they usually look at

- An **overall package**, often a number of GCSEs at grade 4 or 5 including English and maths.
- A **subject grade**, commonly 6 or 7 for subjects that build on GCSE, such as maths, sciences and languages.
- **Predicted grades** at application and actual results in August.

## Why maths and sciences ask for more

A-Level Maths, Further Maths and the sciences step up sharply from GCSE. Sixth forms set higher thresholds so students arrive with solid foundations. See [A-Level Maths tutoring](/tutors/a-level-maths).

## Check early

Look at each sixth form's entry requirements when you shortlist. Our guide to [choosing a sixth form or college](/guides/how-to-choose-a-sixth-form-or-college) lists questions to ask. [Predicted grades](/guides/predicted-grades-explained) often decide conditional offers.

## What if grades fall short?

Speak to the sixth form straight away on results day. Options can include a lower subject, a resit or a different course. See [results day: what to do](/guides/results-day-gcse-a-level-what-to-do).
