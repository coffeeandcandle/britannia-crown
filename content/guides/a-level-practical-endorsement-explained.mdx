In A-Level biology, chemistry and physics, students take part in practical work that is reported separately as the **practical endorsement**. It is **Pass or Not Classified** and does not change the A-Level grade.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- It is reported as **Pass or Not Classified**, separate from A to E grades.
- It is assessed by **teachers** during practical work.
- Some **university science courses** may ask for a Pass.

</Callout>

## How it works

Students carry out a set number of practical activities and show they can meet criteria such as following instructions, recording results and using equipment safely. The teacher records the evidence.

## What it does not do

It does not count towards the grade, and practical skills are tested in the written exams. Students still need to understand the practicals for the papers.

## Why it matters

Some universities ask for a Pass in science subjects, particularly for courses like medicine or the sciences. Check each course's entry requirements. See [A-Level entry requirements](/guides/a-level-entry-requirements-explained).

## Private candidates

Private candidates face extra steps for the practical element, which can make some sciences harder to take privately. See [coursework and non-exam assessment](/guides/coursework-and-non-exam-assessment-explained) and our [A-Level Chemistry tutoring](/tutors/a-level-chemistry).
