An apprenticeship lets young people earn while they learn. It is a real alternative to A-Levels and university, and it is worth understanding how it works.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Apprentices **work, earn a wage and study** towards a qualification.
- Apprenticeships exist at **different levels**, up to degree level.
- They are **competitive**, so apply early and to several.

</Callout>

## How apprenticeships work

An apprentice is employed and works most of the week, with a portion of time given to off-the-job training with a college or training provider. They are paid, and the training leads to a nationally recognised qualification.

## Levels

Apprenticeships exist at several levels, from intermediate (similar to GCSE level) to higher and degree apprenticeships. The level required depends on the job and the applicant's qualifications.

## Who they suit

Apprenticeships suit students who:

- prefer **learning in a workplace** to a classroom;
- want to **earn** straight away;
- know the kind of career they want.

## Entry requirements

Employers set their own. Many ask for GCSEs in English and maths at a certain grade, and some want other qualifications. Higher and degree apprenticeships can need A-Levels or equivalent.

## How to apply

Apprenticeships are advertised on the government's find an apprenticeship service and on employer websites. Applications can include forms, tests and interviews, much like a job. Applying to several is wise.

| Paid? | No | Yes |

## Preparing

Encourage work experience, a clear CV and practice at interviews. Strong GCSEs in English and maths help most.
