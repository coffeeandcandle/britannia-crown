The personal statement is a student's chance to show why they want a course and what they have done to prepare. It must be their own work, but parents can help in valuable ways.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- The statement should be **in the student's own words**.
- Parents help best by **asking questions, reading drafts and keeping deadlines**.
- UCAS has been changing the format, so **check the current rules**.

</Callout>

## What the statement is for

Admissions tutors use it to judge motivation and suitability for the course. They look for genuine interest, relevant experience and evidence of reflection, not a list of achievements.

## A changing format

UCAS has been moving from one long free-form statement to a structured approach with guided questions. Check the UCAS website for the format, questions and length limits that apply for your entry year before your child starts writing.

## How parents can help

- **Ask questions.** Why this subject? What have you read, done or noticed that sparked your interest?
- **Encourage early drafts.** Starting early means more time to improve.
- **Read for clarity**, spelling and tone, but leave the content to your child.
- Suggest they **speak to teachers** and ask for feedback.

## What not to do

- Do not write or heavily rewrite it. Universities check for similarities and for authenticity.
- Do not copy examples from the internet.
- Avoid clichés such as "ever since I was a child".

## Good content

Strong statements show specific interests, not general ones: a book that made them think, a project, work experience or a debate they followed. They explain what the student learned from it, and link it to the course.

## Timeline

Start early in Year 12, gather ideas over the summer, draft in September and finalise well before the school's internal deadline. UCAS deadlines vary by course, with an earlier date for Oxford, Cambridge, medicine, dentistry and veterinary courses. See [Oxbridge applications](/guides/oxbridge-applications-what-parents-can-do).
