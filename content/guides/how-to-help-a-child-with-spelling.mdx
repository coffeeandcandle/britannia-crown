Spelling improves with **short, regular practice** and plenty of reading. These ideas work for most primary-aged children.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **Little and often** works best: five minutes a day.
- Teach **patterns and rules**, not just lists to memorise.
- **Reading widely** helps words look right.

</Callout>

## Practical ideas

- **Look, say, cover, write, check.** Look at the word, say it, cover it, write it from memory, then check.
- **Group words by pattern**, such as -ight or -tion.
- **Break long words into syllables.**
- **Use the words in sentences** to build meaning.
- **Make it playful**, with games, word searches or a spelling jar.

## Reading helps

Children who read often see correct spelling repeatedly. See [how to help your child read at home](/guides/how-to-help-your-child-read-at-home).

## What the school expects

Schools follow spelling rules from phonics onwards, and Year 6 includes a spelling test within the national tests. See [Year 6 grammar, punctuation and spelling](/guides/year-6-grammar-punctuation-and-spelling-explained).

## If spelling stays hard

Talk to the class teacher. Persistent difficulties may point to a learning difference. See [dyslexia support at school and home](/guides/dyslexia-support-at-school-and-home).
