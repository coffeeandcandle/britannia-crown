"SATs" is the everyday name for the **national curriculum tests** children take in primary school in England. The main set are taken at the end of Year 6.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- The main tests are **Key Stage 2 SATs in Year 6, in May**.
- They cover **reading, grammar, punctuation and spelling, and maths**.
- Results are reported as **scaled scores** and teacher assessments.

</Callout>

## What children take

In Year 6, children sit tests in reading, grammar, punctuation and spelling, and maths. Writing is assessed by teachers. Schools report results to parents before the end of the summer term.

## Earlier checks

There are other national checks in primary school, such as the phonics screening check in Year 1 and the multiplication tables check in Year 4. Key Stage 1 tests are optional, and teacher assessment is used. See [Key Stage 1 assessments](/guides/key-stage-1-assessments-explained) and our guides to the [phonics check](/guides/year-1-phonics-screening-check-explained) and [times tables check](/guides/multiplication-tables-check-year-4).

## Do they matter?

SATs measure school performance and show how your child is doing, but they don't decide secondary places. See [how school admissions criteria work](/guides/how-school-admissions-criteria-work). Results do help secondary schools set groups in Year 7.

## Supporting your child

Keep it calm. Short, regular practice and good sleep help more than last-minute cramming. For the detail, read [KS2 SATs explained](/guides/ks2-sats-explained) and [scaled scores explained](/guides/sats-scaled-scores-explained).
