Offer day can bring disappointment. There are things you can do, and many families find that things work out better than they feared.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **Accept the place** you are offered, so you do not lose it.
- Ask to join the **waiting list** for higher preferences.
- Consider an **appeal** if you have strong grounds.

</Callout>

## Why it happens

If more children apply than there are places, schools use their admission criteria. Some families will not get their first preference even if they met the criteria. The council makes an offer based on your ranked list, giving you the highest preference possible.

## Step 1: Accept the place

Accepting the offered school safeguards your child's position. It does not prevent you from taking further steps for another school.

## Step 2: Join waiting lists

Ask the school or local authority to put your child on the waiting list for schools you ranked higher. Lists are ordered by the school's criteria, so a child may move up as places open or if a more highly ranked child joins.

## Step 3: Consider an appeal

You have the right to appeal. Panels look at whether the admission rules were applied properly and whether the school can admit your child without harming education. See [school admission appeals in England](/guides/school-admission-appeals-england).

## Step 4: Plan alternatives

Visit the offered school again, ask questions and think about how to make it work. Many children settle well in schools their parents did not choose first.

## If you are still unhappy

Speak to the school, which may offer a meeting. Look at other options, including independent schools or home education if appropriate. See [independent school entry](/guides/independent-school-entry-assessments-and-bursaries).

## Keep perspective

It is a stressful moment, but it is rarely the end of the story. Staying calm and supportive helps your child.
