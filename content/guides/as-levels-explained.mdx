AS-Levels used to be the first year of an A-Level. Since reforms in England, they have a different role, which confuses many families.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- AS-Levels are **stand-alone qualifications**, roughly half an A-Level in size.
- The AS grade **does not count** towards an A-Level in England.
- Many schools no longer offer them, so check.

</Callout>

## What changed

Previously, the AS exams taken after a year of study counted towards the final A-Level. After reforms, A-Levels are assessed mostly at the end of two years, and AS-Levels became separate qualifications.

## What AS-Levels are used for

- **Breadth:** adding a fourth subject at a lighter level.
- **Trying a subject:** studying something new without committing to two years.
- **A fallback:** some students who find a full A-Level too much move to an AS.

## Do they help with university?

Universities mainly consider A-Level offers. An AS grade can add to a student's profile and, for some qualifications, carries UCAS points, but it rarely replaces an A-Level.

## What schools offer

Practice varies, and many schools do not offer AS-Levels. Ask the sixth form what is available and whether internal exams are used instead.

## Practical points

- AS-Levels still require time and effort, so think about workload.
- Check how they fit with the main subjects.
