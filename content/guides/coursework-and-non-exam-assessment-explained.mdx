Many GCSEs and A-Levels are assessed mainly by exams, but some include work done during the course. Understanding what counts helps families plan time and avoid pitfalls.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **NEA** is assessed work done outside formal exams.
- It appears in **some subjects**, and rules about supervision are strict.
- Plan **time and deadlines** with your child.

</Callout>

## What it is

Non-exam assessment can be a project, practical investigation, performance, portfolio or extended piece of writing. In many subjects it has largely replaced older coursework, with tighter rules about how much help is allowed.

## Where it appears

NEA is common in subjects such as art and design, design and technology, drama, music, PE, computer science and some A-Level subjects. Languages include speaking assessments. Most GCSEs in English, maths and science have little or no NEA, but some include required practicals.

## How it is supervised

Schools must make sure the work is the student's own. Teachers supervise and sign declarations. Using AI tools or copying could lead to a penalty, including disqualification, so make sure your child knows the rules.

## Helping your child

- Know the **deadlines** and break the project into smaller steps.
- Encourage regular progress rather than last-minute rushes.
- Offer encouragement, but do not do the work for them.

## If something goes wrong

Illness or other problems can affect deadlines. Tell the school quickly. Schools may be able to arrange support or special consideration. See [exam access arrangements](/guides/exam-access-arrangements-extra-time).

## Private candidates

Subjects with NEA can be hard to take privately because the work must be supervised. See [entering exams as a private candidate](/guides/private-candidate-gcse-how-to-enter).
