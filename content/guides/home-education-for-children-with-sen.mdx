Some families choose home education because school has not met their child's needs. It can work, but it needs careful thought.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- The **legal duty** to provide suitable education applies, taking into account your child's needs.
- Speak to the **local authority** if your child has an EHCP.
- Seek **independent advice** and support.

</Callout>

## The legal position

Parents must ensure that education is suitable for the child's age, ability, aptitude and any special educational needs. You can meet this duty through home education. See [home education and the law](/guides/home-education-legal-requirements-england).

## If your child has an EHCP

The plan remains in place when a child is home educated, but what happens to the support depends on the local authority and the plan. Speak to the council before deregistering, and ask how the plan will be reviewed and what is available. See [SEN support and EHCPs explained](/guides/sen-support-and-ehcps-explained).

## What home education can offer

Flexible pacing, one-to-one attention, tailored approaches and a calmer environment can help some children. It also asks a lot of parents, who may need training or specialist support.

## Support to consider

- Specialist tutors, with experience of your child's needs.
- Therapies or assessments, arranged privately or through the NHS.
- Parent support groups and charities.
- Sensory-friendly or small-group activities.

## Exams

Children with needs may be eligible for exam adjustments, which may need evidence and an exam centre that can arrange them. See [exam access arrangements](/guides/exam-access-arrangements-extra-time).

## Review

Check regularly whether your child is making progress and whether the arrangement is working. See [dyslexia support at school and home](/guides/dyslexia-support-at-school-and-home) for an example of practical help.
