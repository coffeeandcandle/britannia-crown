Many children go through phases of disliking reading. With patience and the right approach, most rediscover it.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Find out **why** reading feels hard or dull.
- Offer **choice**: comics, non-fiction, audiobooks and series.
- Make reading **warm and low pressure**.

</Callout>

## Why children go off reading

- Books that are too hard or too easy.
- A hidden difficulty, such as dyslexia or vision problems.
- Reading seen as work rather than pleasure.
- Competing attractions, like screens.

## Check for difficulties

If effort and unhappiness seem out of proportion, speak to the teacher and consider a vision check. See [dyslexia support at school and home](/guides/dyslexia-support-at-school-and-home).

## Make it enjoyable

- Let your child choose from a wide range of books.
- Try **series, joke books, comics, graphic novels and magazines**.
- Read the same book together and talk about it.
- Use audiobooks while following the text.

## Link to interests

Pick books about football, animals, space, cooking or whatever your child loves. Interest drives effort.

## Set a gentle routine

A short, regular reading time works better than an argument. Read together, and keep it relaxed. Let children see you reading too.

## Celebrate effort

Notice progress and comment on what they enjoyed. Avoid comparing with siblings.

## When to seek help

If your child remains reluctant and is falling behind, ask the school about support. A tutor can offer patient, structured help. See [how to help your child read at home](/guides/how-to-help-your-child-read-at-home).
