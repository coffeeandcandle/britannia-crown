Armed forces families move often, sometimes at short notice. The admissions system makes some allowances, and knowing them helps.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- You can usually **apply before moving** with official evidence.
- Some schools **prioritise** service children.
- Plan for **in-year moves** and keep records.

</Callout>

## Applying before you move

The national admissions code allows service families to apply for places in advance, using a posting order or a letter from a commanding officer. Councils should not refuse an application only because the family has not yet moved.

## Priority

Admission authorities can give priority to children of service personnel, and many do. Check the admission policy of each school, including how to provide evidence.

## In-year moves

Moves often happen mid-year, so the in-year process is important. See [in-year school admissions](/guides/in-year-school-admissions-moving-mid-year). Contact the council as soon as you have a posting date, and ask about waiting lists and school places in the new area.

## Support for children

Children who move often may need help with gaps in learning, friendships and settling in. Keep copies of reports and assessments, and speak to the new school early about what support is available.

## Boarding and continuity

Some families choose boarding schools to give children stability while parents are posted. Allowances may be available. Ask your unit for current support. See [boarding schools in the UK](/guides/boarding-schools-in-the-uk).

## Overseas postings

If you are returning to the UK after a posting, see [returning to the UK with children](/guides/returning-to-the-uk-with-children-school-places).

## Useful steps

- Contact the council early.
- Provide posting evidence.
- Visit schools online if you cannot in person.
- Keep your child's records together.
