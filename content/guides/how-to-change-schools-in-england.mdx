Sometimes a school is not working for a child. Changing is possible, but a good plan makes it smoother.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Try to **resolve problems** with the current school first, if you can.
- Apply through the **in-year process**.
- Plan the **move and transfer** carefully.

</Callout>

## Why families change

Common reasons include moving house, bullying or unhappiness, mismatched learning needs, a better fit elsewhere or changes to a family's circumstances.

## Step 1: Talk to the school

If there is a problem, raise it with the class teacher, head of year or head teacher. Sometimes an issue can be solved, and if not, the conversation helps you decide.

## Step 2: Research alternatives

Look at schools with places, and visit. Consider travel, ethos and how they would support your child. See [how to choose a secondary school](/guides/how-to-choose-a-secondary-school).

## Step 3: Apply

For a mid-year move, you apply to the local authority or directly to the school, depending on the school type. See [in-year school admissions](/guides/in-year-school-admissions-moving-mid-year). If you are refused, you can usually appeal. See [school admission appeals](/guides/school-admission-appeals-england).

## Step 4: Tell the current school

Once a place is confirmed, inform the current school in writing and agree a leaving date. Ask for records to be passed on.

## Helping your child settle

- Prepare your child with visits and stories of the new school.
- Share any relevant information about their needs with the new school.
- Expect a settling-in period and keep in touch with staff.
- Maintain friendships from the old school where possible.

## Gaps in learning

Different schools teach topics in different orders, so there may be gaps. A tutor can help bridge them. See [tutoring](/services/tutoring).
