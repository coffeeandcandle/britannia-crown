After GCSEs, students often choose between A-Levels and a BTEC. They are different kinds of qualification, and the right choice depends on how your child learns and what they want to do next.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **A-Levels** are mostly assessed by final exams and are subject based.
- **BTECs** are more practical and assessed through assignments and projects, as well as some exams.
- Check **university and employer requirements** before choosing.

</Callout>

## A-Levels

A-Levels focus on academic subjects, such as maths, history or chemistry. Most are assessed mainly by exams at the end of two years. They suit students who like in-depth study of a subject and are comfortable with exams.

## BTECs

BTECs are vocational and applied qualifications, in areas such as business, health, sport or computing. They are usually assessed by coursework, projects and assignments, sometimes alongside exams. They suit students who prefer learning by doing and sustained project work.

## How universities see them

Many universities accept BTECs, either alone or alongside A-Levels. Some courses, such as medicine and some sciences, usually need specific A-Levels. Check entry requirements on the university website before choosing.

## Changes to qualifications

The government has been reforming post-16 qualifications, which affects which BTECs and other courses are available. Check with the provider and the Department for Education for current information. See [T Levels explained](/guides/t-levels-explained).

## Questions to ask

- What do you want to do after sixth form: university, apprenticeship or work?
- What are the **entry requirements** for that route?
- Do you prefer **exams or coursework**?
- Which options does your chosen college or sixth form offer?

## Getting advice

Talk to careers advisers at school, and to the colleges themselves. Look at open days. A consultant can help you compare routes. See [how to choose a sixth form or college](/guides/how-to-choose-a-sixth-form-or-college).
