School readiness is often described in terms of reading and counting, but schools usually care more about confidence, independence and communication.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Readiness is mostly about **independence, communication and social skills**.
- Children do **not** need to read or write before Reception.
- Help gently, and speak to the school about needs.

</Callout>

## What schools look for

- **Self-care:** managing coats, shoes, toilet and eating with help when needed.
- **Communication:** listening, speaking in sentences, asking for help.
- **Social skills:** playing, sharing and coping with changes.
- **Curiosity:** enjoying stories, songs and exploring.

## What helps at home

- Talk about everything: what you can see, what you are doing, what will happen.
- Read stories every day.
- Practise dressing, using cutlery and washing hands.
- Play turn-taking games.
- Visit the school and talk about what it will be like.

## What is not needed

Children do not need to read, write full sentences or do sums. Teachers expect to teach these from the start, and children arrive with very different experiences.

## If your child has additional needs

Tell the school and nursery early. They can plan support, and you can ask for a meeting before term starts. See also [SEN support and EHCPs explained](/guides/sen-support-and-ehcps-explained).

## Younger children

Summer-born children and those who have had less nursery may need extra time to settle. Teachers are used to this. See [summer-born children and starting school](/guides/summer-born-children-and-starting-school).

## Looking after yourself

Many parents feel anxious. Staying calm and positive helps your child.
