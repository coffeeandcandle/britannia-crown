Secondary school league tables use measures called Progress 8 and Attainment 8. They can look puzzling, but a simple explanation helps.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **Attainment 8** shows the average grades pupils reach.
- **Progress 8** shows how much progress pupils make from primary school.
- A Progress 8 score of **0** is about average.

</Callout>

## Attainment 8

Attainment 8 adds up a pupil's results in eight subjects: English and maths (counted double), three other EBacc-style subjects and three other approved subjects. A school's score is the average across its pupils. Higher scores mean higher average grades.

## Progress 8

Progress 8 compares pupils' GCSE results with those of pupils nationally who got the same results at the end of primary school. If pupils do better than similar pupils, the school's Progress 8 is above zero. If they do worse, it is below.

## Why it matters

Progress 8 helps show how well a school helps children improve, not just which school has pupils who started strong. A school with a modest intake can still have an excellent score.

## Reading the numbers

- Scores close to zero mean about average progress.
- Check the **confidence interval** the government publishes, because small schools' scores can swing from year to year.
- Look at trends across several years, not a single result.

## Limits

These figures do not cover wellbeing, pastoral care, the arts, sport or how a school treats children with particular needs. They also take no account of what is best for your child.

## Where to find them

The Department for Education publishes school performance data on its compare school and college performance service. Use it alongside inspection reports. See [Ofsted ratings explained](/guides/ofsted-ratings-explained).
