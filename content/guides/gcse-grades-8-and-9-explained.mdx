GCSE grades are numbered **9 to 1**, with 9 the highest. Grades 8 and 9 sit at the top of the scale.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- **Grade 9** is above the old A*, awarded to a small share of top students.
- **Grade 8** is between an A and an A*.
- **Grade 7** is equivalent to the old A.

</Callout>

## How they compare with the old grades

| New grade | Old equivalent |
|---|---|
| 9 | Above A* |
| 8 | Between A and A* |
| 7 | A |
| 6 | Between B and A |
| 5 | Between C and B |
| 4 | C |

Grades 8 and 9 were introduced so that exam boards could separate the highest performers.

## How hard is it to get a 9?

A 9 is deliberately demanding and only a small share of students achieve it in any subject. It tends to require near-perfect performance on the hardest questions.

## Do they matter?

Highly selective sixth forms and universities may look at a strong GCSE profile, but entry rules vary. See [A-Level entry requirements](/guides/a-level-entry-requirements-explained).
