Letting someone teach your child, at home or online, is a matter of trust. Asking sensible questions about safeguarding is normal, and good tutors and agencies expect it.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Ask about **DBS checks, references and safeguarding training**.
- Know how **online lessons** are run and recorded.
- Trust your instincts and **ask for changes** if anything feels wrong.

</Callout>

## Questions about checks

- Do you hold an **enhanced DBS certificate**? When was it issued, and are you on the update service?
- Can you provide **references**, ideally from other families or schools?
- Have you had **safeguarding training**, and when?
- Can you confirm your **identity and qualifications**?

For private tutors who work independently, there is no single legal requirement for checks, which is why asking matters. Agencies normally carry out checks as part of their process, but ask what they cover.

## Questions about how lessons run

- Where will lessons take place? A shared space in the home is best.
- For online lessons, **which platform** is used, and are sessions recorded?
- How does the tutor communicate with your child outside lessons? Messages are best sent to parents or through an agency.
- What is the plan for **cancellation, lateness or emergencies**?

## Practical safeguards at home

- Be in the house during in-person lessons and agree that the door stays open or the lesson takes place in a shared room.
- Be nearby for online lessons with younger children.
- Make sure your child knows they can tell you anything that makes them uncomfortable.

## Online safety

Use the tutor's agreed platform. Avoid sharing personal social media accounts. Keep any recordings secure and agree how long they are kept.

## If something feels wrong

Trust your instincts. Pause the lessons, talk to the agency if there is one and seek advice. If you think a child is at risk of harm, contact the police or your local authority children's services.
