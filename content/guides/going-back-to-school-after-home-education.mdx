Families often return to school after a period of home education, for many reasons. A good plan helps the move.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Apply through **in-year admissions** if outside the normal round.
- Share your **child's records and learning**.
- Expect a **settling-in period**.

</Callout>

## Applying for a place

For a mid-year move, contact your local council or the schools you like. See [in-year school admissions](/guides/in-year-school-admissions-moving-mid-year). Places are not guaranteed, and waiting lists may apply. If refused, you can appeal.

## Talking to the school

Explain your child's learning, strengths and any gaps. Bring examples of work, reports and any exam results. A meeting with the head or SENCO can help.

## Preparing your child

- Visit the school and meet staff.
- Talk about routines such as timetables, uniform and lunch.
- Practise getting up and ready at school time before starting.
- Prepare for different ways of learning, such as classroom discussion.

## Gaps and strengths

Home educated children often have uneven learning profiles, with strengths in some areas and gaps in others, such as specific topics or exam technique. A tutor can help bridge gaps. See [how to change schools in England](/guides/how-to-change-schools-in-england).

## Friendships

Joining clubs and talking about friendships helps. Reassure your child that settling takes time.

## Keeping an eye

Check in regularly with your child and with teachers. Raise concerns early.

## Exam years

If returning in Year 10 or 11, check which exam boards the school uses and whether your child can complete courses in time. See [qualifications for home educated children](/guides/qualifications-for-home-educated-children).
