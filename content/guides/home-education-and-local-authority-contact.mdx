After deregistering, many families hear from their local authority. How you respond can set the tone for the relationship.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Councils must **identify children not receiving suitable education**.
- You can respond **in writing** and share reports or work samples.
- A **polite, informed** approach usually works best.

</Callout>

## Why they contact you

Local authorities have a duty to make arrangements to identify children not receiving suitable education. They typically send a letter after a child leaves school, and may follow up annually.

## What they can ask

Councils can make informal enquiries. They may ask for information about your provision. Many home educators share a short report, a sample of work or a summary of their approach and resources.

## What you are not required to do

- Allow a home visit.
- Meet in person.
- Follow the national curriculum or school-style timetable.

You can offer alternatives that show suitable education in other ways.

## Good practice

- Reply in writing, courteously and on time.
- Describe your approach, resources and the child's activities and interests.
- Include examples of work, photos or outings.
- Keep copies of what you send.

## If the council has concerns

If a council believes education is not suitable, it can take steps under the law. Keep records of your provision to help show what you are doing. Get advice from home education support groups if you are worried.

## Rules may change

Proposals for new duties and registers have been discussed. Check gov.uk for current rules. See [home education and the law](/guides/home-education-legal-requirements-england).
