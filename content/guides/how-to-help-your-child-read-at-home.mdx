Reading at home is one of the most valuable things a family can do. It does not need special skills, just time and warmth.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Read **a little every day**, ideally at a regular time.
- **Talk** about what you read.
- Keep it **enjoyable**, and let your child choose books.

</Callout>

## Make time

Pick a quiet time, such as before bed or after dinner. Ten to twenty minutes is enough. Regular habits matter more than length.

## Share the reading

For younger children, take turns: you read a page, they read a page. For fluent readers, read aloud in different voices, or let them read to a younger sibling or pet.

## Talk about the book

- Ask what they think will happen next.
- Ask about characters' feelings.
- Link to their own life: "That reminds me of...".
- Explain new words naturally.

## Help with tricky words

If your child gets stuck, encourage them to sound out the word, then give help if needed. Do not turn each page into a test.

## Choose books they enjoy

Include stories, non-fiction, comics, poetry and joke books. Visit the library often. Let your child choose some of the books.

## Reading aloud still matters

Even when children can read alone, being read to builds vocabulary and attention. Audiobooks help too.

## When there are difficulties

If reading is a struggle, speak to the teacher. Persistent difficulty may need extra support. See [dyslexia support](/guides/dyslexia-support-at-school-and-home) and [phonics and learning to read](/guides/phonics-and-learning-to-read-in-england).
