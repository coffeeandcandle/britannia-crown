Dyslexia is a common difference in how people learn to read and spell. Early understanding and the right support make a big difference.

<Callout title="In a nutshell">

- Speak to the **school and SENCO** if you have concerns.
- A formal **assessment** can confirm needs and guide support.
- Build **confidence** as well as skills.

</Callout>

## Signs to look for

- Slow, effortful reading and spelling mistakes that continue despite practice.
- Difficulty with sounds and letters, or with remembering sequences.
- Avoiding reading or writing, or very tiring homework.
- Strong ideas and speech, but weaker writing.

These signs do not prove dyslexia, so professional advice is needed.

## Getting help

Start with the class teacher and SENCO. The school may put support in place without a formal diagnosis. If you want a full assessment, ask about options. A qualified assessor can produce a report with recommendations. See [SEN support and EHCPs](/guides/sen-support-and-ehcps-explained).

## What helps in school

- Structured, multi-sensory teaching of reading and spelling.
- Extra time and adjustments where appropriate.
- Using technology such as spell checkers or text-to-speech.
- Positive teachers who recognise strengths.

## At home

- Read together daily, taking turns.
- Use audiobooks to keep stories and vocabulary growing.
- Praise effort and strengths.
- Keep homework in short blocks with breaks.

## Tutoring

A tutor with relevant experience can offer structured practice and build confidence. Choose someone who has worked with dyslexic learners, and share any assessment report. See [phonics and learning to read](/guides/phonics-and-learning-to-read-in-england).

## Exams

Students may be eligible for access arrangements such as extra time, based on evidence. See [exam access arrangements](/guides/exam-access-arrangements-extra-time).
