Exam boards publish their **summer timetables well before the exams**. Checking early helps students plan revision around the actual dates.

<Callout title="At a glance">

- Timetables are published by each **exam board**, and schools share the final version.
- Students with several subjects may have **clashes or gaps** that affect planning.
- Use the timetable to build a **revision plan working backwards** from each exam.

</Callout>

## Why check early

Seeing the real dates makes revision concrete. It shows which subjects come first, where there are gaps and which days are heavy.

## What to plan

- Put every exam in a shared family calendar.
- Allow the most time for the hardest or earliest papers.
- Plan rest days, particularly before double-exam days.

## Where to find dates

Ask the school for your child's personal timetable, and check the exam boards' websites. See [when are GCSE exams](/guides/when-are-gcse-exams) and [when are A-Level exams](/guides/when-are-a-level-exams).

Then build a plan: [how to build a revision timetable](/guides/how-to-build-a-revision-timetable).
