Year 11 mock results are a snapshot, not a verdict. Used well, they show **what to work on while there is still time**.

<Callout title="At a glance">

- Mocks **do not count** towards final grades, but they shape predicted grades.
- Look at **where marks were lost**, not only the grade.
- Turn each subject's results into **two or three priorities**.

</Callout>

## Look beyond the grade

Ask which questions or topics lost marks, and whether the cause was knowledge, misreading questions or timing. Ask the school for a marked paper or feedback.

## Predicted grades

Schools often use mock results to produce predicted grades for sixth form and college applications. See [predicted grades explained](/guides/predicted-grades-explained).

## Make a plan

List priorities for each subject and slot them into a revision timetable. See [how to build a revision timetable](/guides/how-to-build-a-revision-timetable) and [how to use past papers](/guides/how-to-use-past-papers).

For the full approach, read [how to use mock exams well](/guides/gcse-mock-exams-how-to-use-them).
