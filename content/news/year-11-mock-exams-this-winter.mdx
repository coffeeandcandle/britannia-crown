Many schools hold GCSE mock exams in the winter of Year 11, often in December or January. Dates vary, so check your school's calendar.

<Callout title="At a glance">

- Mocks do **not count** towards final grades.
- They often shape **predicted grades** used for sixth form applications.
- The best use is to **find and fix gaps**.

</Callout>

## Preparing

- Build a short **revision timetable**. See [how to build a revision timetable](/guides/how-to-build-a-revision-timetable).
- Practise with **past papers** under timed conditions.
- Protect sleep, meals and breaks.

## After the mocks

Ask the school for marked papers and feedback. List topics where marks were lost and slot them into the plan. See [GCSE mock exams: how to use them well](/guides/gcse-mock-exams-how-to-use-them).

## Looking after confidence

A disappointing result is information, not a verdict. See [how to support a child through exam stress](/guides/how-to-support-a-child-through-exam-stress).

<Cta>

A tutor can help turn mock results into a clear plan.

</Cta>
